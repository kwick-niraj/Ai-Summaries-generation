## Introduction

Across the sands of seventh-century Arabia, a profound transformation unfolds as the life of Muhammad, the final Prophet of Islam, shapes the destiny of nations and the hearts of millions. The Sealed Nectar by Safiur Rahman Mubarakpuri immerses itself in the intricate tapestry of Muhammad’s journey—from his noble lineage and early years in Mecca, through the trials of prophethood, to the establishment of a vibrant, unified community in Medina. The narrative traces the challenges, revelations, and pivotal moments that defined his mission, illuminating the spiritual, social, and political landscapes that he navigated with unwavering faith and compassion.

As the story progresses, the contours of Muhammad’s character—his patience in adversity, wisdom in leadership, and mercy toward friend and foe—emerge with clarity. The unfolding events reveal not only the external struggles against opposition and hardship but also the internal resolve that inspired a movement rooted in justice, humility, and devotion. Through victories and setbacks, alliances and betrayals, the journey culminates in a legacy that continues to resonate across centuries, inviting reflection on the enduring values and lessons that shaped a civilization. The path from revelation to realization offers a window into the heart of Islam and the remarkable life at its center.

## Chapter 1 Roots, Upbringing, and the World Before Revelation

Before the world would know the name Muhammad, the land of Arabia was a place of shifting sands and ancient customs. Life there was shaped by tribal loyalties, old rivalries, and a deep sense of belonging to one’s people. The city of Mecca, nestled among rocky hills, was a crossroads for traders and pilgrims alike. Its heart beat around the Ka’bah, a sanctuary that drew people from far and wide, even as the meaning of its rituals had faded into a tangle of inherited traditions.

In this world, family meant everything. People clung to their lineages, tracing their roots back through generations, each name a link in a chain of honor and memory. The tribe of Quraysh, respected and influential, held guardianship over the Ka’bah. Among them, a child was born into the clan of Hashim—a child whose arrival was marked by both hope and hardship.

His father, a young man known for his honesty and gentle nature, passed away before he could meet his son. The boy’s mother, left to raise him alone, carried the weight of both love and loss. She named him Muhammad, a name that would one day echo across continents. In his earliest days, he was entrusted to a foster family in the desert, as was the custom. There, under the open sky, he learned the rhythms of nature and the value of simplicity. The desert’s silence became his first teacher, and the kindness of his caretakers left a mark that would never fade.

Tragedy struck again when his mother died during a journey, leaving him an orphan at a tender age. He was taken in by his grandfather, a figure of dignity and wisdom, who offered both protection and affection. But this comfort, too, was short-lived. Soon, the boy found himself in the care of his uncle, a man of modest means but unwavering loyalty. Through these early losses, he learned resilience—not the kind that hardens the heart, but the kind that deepens empathy.

Growing up, Muhammad was known for his trustworthiness. People in Mecca, whether rich or poor, would seek him out to settle disputes or safeguard their valuables. He worked as a shepherd, spending long hours alone with his thoughts and the animals in his care. Later, he joined the bustling world of trade, traveling with caravans and earning a reputation for fairness. In a society where deception was common, his honesty stood out.

The world around him was changing, but not always for the better. The old values of generosity and hospitality were being overshadowed by greed and pride. The strong often took advantage of the weak, and the bonds of kinship were sometimes twisted into excuses for injustice. Yet, even as a young man, Muhammad seemed to move through these challenges with a quiet integrity. He was neither drawn to the excesses of his peers nor quick to judge them. Instead, he carried himself with a calm dignity that made others take notice.

It was during this time that a wealthy widow, known for her intelligence and independence, asked him to manage her trading ventures. Impressed by his character, she later proposed marriage. Their partnership was built on mutual respect and affection, providing him with stability and support. Together, they raised a family and built a home that became a refuge for those in need.

Looking back, it’s easy to see how these early experiences shaped the man Muhammad would become. The pain of loss, the lessons of the desert, the trust of his community, and the love of his family—all these threads wove together to form a foundation of strength and compassion. Most people, faced with such hardship, might have grown bitter or withdrawn. But for Muhammad, each trial seemed to deepen his sense of purpose and his commitment to justice.

In the quiet moments before dawn, he would often retreat to the hills outside Mecca, seeking solitude and reflection. The world was full of noise and confusion, but in those silent hours, he searched for meaning beyond the rituals and customs of his people. It was as if he sensed that something greater was waiting—something that would call him to a life unlike any other.

## Chapter 2 The First Revelation and the Challenge of a New Message

There are moments in life when everything changes in an instant. For Muhammad, that moment came in the stillness of a cave on the outskirts of Mecca. He had grown accustomed to seeking solitude there, away from the bustle of the city and the weight of expectation. The cave became a sanctuary—a place to ponder the mysteries of existence and the suffering he saw around him.

One night, as he sat in contemplation, a presence filled the space. It was overwhelming, both terrifying and awe-inspiring. A command was given: to read, to recite, to bear witness to a message that would upend the world he knew. In that moment, fear and uncertainty washed over him. He returned home shaken, seeking comfort in the arms of his wife, who reassured him and sought counsel from those she trusted.

The days that followed were filled with questions. What did this experience mean? Why had he been chosen? The answers did not come easily. The message he received was clear: there is only one God, and people are called to live with justice, compassion, and humility. But sharing this message would not be simple. The society around him was deeply attached to its traditions, its idols, and its sense of superiority.

At first, the call was quiet. Only a handful of close friends and family members knew of it. They were drawn not by promises of wealth or power, but by the sincerity and clarity of the message. Among them were people from all walks of life—those who had little to lose, and those who risked everything by believing. Their faith was tested from the very beginning. The powerful in Mecca saw the new message as a threat to their authority and way of life. They responded with ridicule, then with hostility.

It’s easy to underestimate the courage it took to stand firm in the face of such opposition. The early followers faced insults, social isolation, and even violence. Some lost their livelihoods; others were tortured for refusing to abandon their beliefs. Yet, they found strength in their conviction and in the example set by Muhammad. He did not respond to hatred with anger. Instead, he showed patience, kindness, and a willingness to forgive.

There was a moment when the pressure became almost unbearable. The leaders of Mecca offered Muhammad wealth, status, and power if he would only abandon his message. Many in his position might have been tempted, but he refused. For him, the truth was not something to be bargained away. He reminded his followers that hardship was part of the journey, and that perseverance would bring its own reward.

As the message spread, it began to attract people from the margins of society—those who had been overlooked or mistreated. They found hope in the promise of a community built on equality and compassion. But this only increased the hostility of those in power. The persecution grew more intense, and some of the early believers were forced to seek refuge in distant lands. Even then, Muhammad encouraged them to remain steadfast, trusting that justice would prevail in the end.

Through it all, he continued to reach out to his people with gentleness and wisdom. He spoke of mercy, forgiveness, and the importance of caring for the vulnerable. He reminded them that true strength lay not in domination, but in self-restraint and humility. Most people, when faced with relentless opposition, would have given up or lashed out. But Muhammad’s response was always measured, always rooted in a deep sense of purpose.

Looking back, it’s clear that these early years were a test of character as much as faith. The challenges were real, and the cost of belief was high. Yet, the small community that formed around Muhammad was bound together by a sense of shared mission and mutual support. They learned to rely on one another, to forgive each other’s shortcomings, and to find hope even in the darkest moments.

## Chapter 3 Enduring Persecution and Building a Community of Faith

As the message of Islam began to take root, the opposition in Mecca grew fiercer. The leaders of the city, threatened by the call to abandon their idols and embrace a new way of life, resorted to increasingly harsh tactics. They imposed social boycotts, cutting off the small band of believers from trade and support. Families were torn apart, friendships tested, and the bonds of community stretched to their limits.

In the midst of this hardship, the believers found unexpected sources of strength. The sense of belonging that came from shared faith became a lifeline. People who had once been strangers now looked out for one another, sharing what little they had and offering comfort in times of distress. The home of Muhammad became a gathering place—a safe haven where people could speak freely and find encouragement.

There were moments when the suffering seemed unbearable. Some of the most vulnerable members of the community faced brutal treatment at the hands of their oppressors. Yet, even in the face of cruelty, Muhammad urged his followers to respond with patience and dignity. He reminded them that their struggle was not just for themselves, but for a greater cause. The example he set—enduring hardship without bitterness, forgiving those who wronged him—became a source of inspiration.

During this period, a few influential figures in Mecca began to take notice. Some were moved by the integrity and resilience of the believers; others were drawn by the beauty of the message itself. Their conversion brought hope, but also intensified the backlash. The leaders of Mecca saw that their grip on power was slipping, and they redoubled their efforts to stamp out the new faith.

When the persecution reached its peak, Muhammad advised some of his followers to seek refuge in a distant land known for its justice and tolerance. The journey was long and uncertain, but those who made it found safety and acceptance. Their experience became a reminder that compassion and fairness could be found even in unexpected places.

Back in Mecca, the situation grew more desperate. The boycott imposed on Muhammad’s clan left them isolated and struggling to survive. Food became scarce, and the threat of violence was ever-present. Yet, through it all, the believers remained steadfast. They supported one another, shared what little they had, and refused to abandon their principles.

It was during these darkest days that Muhammad’s character shone brightest. He continued to reach out to his opponents, seeking reconciliation and understanding. He never lost sight of the humanity of those who opposed him, and he encouraged his followers to do the same. Most people, faced with such relentless hostility, would have become hardened or vengeful. But Muhammad’s response was always rooted in compassion and hope.

Eventually, the boycott was lifted, and the community began to rebuild. The experience had left its mark, but it had also forged a sense of unity and purpose. The believers emerged stronger, more committed to their mission, and more aware of the importance of supporting one another.

In the years that followed, Muhammad faced personal losses that would have broken many. The death of his beloved wife and his loyal uncle left him without two of his closest supporters. Grief weighed heavily on him, but he did not allow it to turn into despair. Instead, he found solace in prayer and in the company of his companions.

Through these trials, the foundations of the Muslim community were laid. The lessons learned during this time—patience in adversity, forgiveness in the face of injustice, and the importance of community—became central to the teachings of Islam. The believers learned that faith was not just a matter of words, but of action. It was something to be lived, even when the cost was high.

Looking back, it’s clear that these years of hardship were not wasted. They shaped the character of the community and prepared them for the challenges that lay ahead. The bonds forged in adversity would become the bedrock of a movement that would one day change the course of history.

## Chapter 4 Migration and the Birth of a New Society

There comes a point when staying in a place becomes impossible, no matter how deep the roots. For Muhammad and his followers, that moment arrived when the hostility in Mecca reached a breaking point. The leaders of the city, determined to silence the message of Islam once and for all, plotted to take his life. The threat was real, and the need for a new beginning became undeniable.

The decision to leave Mecca was not made lightly. The city was home, filled with memories and meaning. But the safety of the community came first. Quietly, plans were made for the believers to slip away, one by one, to a distant city that had offered them refuge. The journey was fraught with danger, and the risk of discovery was ever-present. Yet, hope outweighed fear.

The city that welcomed them was known as Yathrib, later called Medina. Its people had heard of Muhammad’s message and were eager for a leader who could bring peace to their divided community. When he arrived, he was greeted not as a stranger, but as a source of hope. The sense of relief was palpable—a new chapter was beginning.

Building a new society from the ground up was no small task. The people of Medina came from different backgrounds, with old rivalries and grudges simmering beneath the surface. Muhammad’s first priority was to establish a sense of unity. He brought together the newcomers and the locals, encouraging them to see themselves as one community. Agreements were made to ensure mutual support and protection, regardless of tribal affiliation or previous conflicts.

The bonds of brotherhood that formed during this time were remarkable. People who had once been strangers now shared their homes and resources, looking out for one another in ways that went beyond mere obligation. The sense of belonging was real, and it gave the community a strength that would prove essential in the years to come.

But the challenges were far from over. The move to Medina did not end the hostility from Mecca. The leaders of the old city saw the growing strength of the Muslim community as a threat, and they were determined to crush it. Skirmishes and battles followed, testing the resolve of the believers and the wisdom of their leader.

In these moments of crisis, Muhammad’s approach was always measured. He sought to avoid conflict whenever possible, but he was not afraid to defend his community when necessary. His leadership was marked by a careful balance of firmness and mercy. He set clear rules for conduct in battle, emphasizing the importance of restraint and the protection of non-combatants.

The early victories and setbacks in these confrontations taught the community important lessons. They learned the value of discipline, the importance of unity, and the need for trust in their leader. Most people, faced with the uncertainty of war, would have been consumed by fear or anger. But Muhammad encouraged his followers to remain calm, to trust in a higher purpose, and to treat even their enemies with dignity.

As the community grew, new challenges emerged. The influx of new members brought both opportunities and tensions. Disagreements arose, and mistakes were made. But through it all, Muhammad remained a source of guidance and stability. He listened to concerns, mediated disputes, and reminded people of the values that had brought them together.

The society that took shape in Medina was unlike anything the region had seen before. It was built on principles of justice, compassion, and mutual support. The rights of women, children, and the vulnerable were protected. Agreements were made with neighboring communities to ensure peace and cooperation. The sense of purpose that had sustained the believers through years of hardship now found expression in the daily life of a thriving community.

Looking back, it’s clear that the migration to Medina was more than just a change of location. It was the birth of a new way of living—a society built on faith, hope, and the belief that people could rise above their differences to create something better. The lessons learned during this time would shape the future of Islam and leave a legacy that endures to this day.

## Chapter 5 Trials, Triumphs, and the Expansion of the Message

The years in Medina were marked by both hardship and hope. The community, though stronger than before, faced constant threats from outside and within. The leaders of Mecca, unwilling to accept the new reality, launched repeated attacks. Each confrontation brought its own challenges, testing the resolve and unity of the believers.

One of the earliest and most significant battles was fought against overwhelming odds. The community, outnumbered and under-equipped, stood together in defense of their new home. The outcome was a surprise to many—a victory that boosted morale and strengthened faith. But success brought new challenges. Some began to grow overconfident, forgetting the lessons of humility and reliance on one another.

Not every battle ended in triumph. There were moments of loss and heartbreak, times when mistakes were made and the cost was high. In these moments, Muhammad’s leadership was crucial. He did not shy away from acknowledging errors or comforting those who grieved. He reminded his followers that setbacks were part of the journey, and that true strength lay in learning from failure.

The community also faced challenges from within. Not everyone who joined the new society was sincere in their commitment. Some sought to undermine the unity of the believers, spreading rumors and sowing discord. Muhammad responded with patience and wisdom, addressing concerns openly and encouraging honesty. He understood that building trust took time, and that forgiveness was often the best path forward.

As the message of Islam spread, new groups and tribes began to take notice. Some approached with curiosity, others with suspicion. Muhammad welcomed dialogue, seeking common ground and building alliances where possible. He emphasized the importance of justice and fairness in all dealings, regardless of background or belief.

The society in Medina became a model for others. People saw that it was possible to live together in peace, to resolve disputes without violence, and to care for the vulnerable. The principles of charity, honesty, and mutual respect became the foundation of daily life. Most people, when given power, are tempted to use it for their own benefit. But Muhammad used his influence to serve others, always mindful of the trust placed in him.

There were moments of great joy as well. The community celebrated milestones, welcomed new members, and found comfort in shared rituals. The bonds of friendship and family grew stronger, and the sense of purpose that had sustained them through hardship now brought a sense of fulfillment.

Yet, the challenges never disappeared entirely. The threat from Mecca remained, and the need for vigilance was constant. Muhammad continued to lead by example, showing that perseverance, compassion, and faith could overcome even the greatest obstacles.

Looking back, it’s clear that the years in Medina were a time of transformation. The small band of believers who had once faced persecution and isolation had become a thriving community, united by a shared vision and a commitment to justice. The lessons learned during this time—about leadership, forgiveness, and the power of faith—would shape the future of Islam and inspire generations to come.

## Chapter 6 The Turning Tide—From Defense to Reconciliation

In the years that followed, the city of Medina became a place where hope and anxiety often walked hand in hand. The believers had found a home, but the shadow of conflict lingered. The leaders of Mecca, still unwilling to accept the new order, continued their campaigns—sometimes with swords, sometimes with words. Each season brought rumors of alliances and threats, and the community learned to live with uncertainty as a constant companion.

Yet, something was changing. The message of Islam, once whispered in secret, now traveled along the trade routes and through the hearts of travelers. People from distant tribes arrived in Medina, curious about the new way of life they had heard about. Some came with skepticism, others with open minds. They found a society that, despite its struggles, was marked by fairness and a sense of belonging.

One day, a group of pilgrims from Mecca arrived, seeking to perform the ancient rites at the Ka’bah. The tension was thick—old wounds had not healed, and trust was fragile. But Muhammad saw an opportunity for something greater. He met with the leaders of Mecca, not as an enemy, but as a neighbor longing for peace. The negotiations were tense, with each side wary of betrayal. In the end, an agreement was reached: the Muslims would return home that year, but would be allowed to perform their pilgrimage the next.

To some, this felt like a defeat. The terms seemed to favor the Meccans, and disappointment rippled through the community. But Muhammad’s calm assurance never wavered. He saw the bigger picture—a chance to show the world that peace was possible, even between bitter rivals. Most people, when faced with humiliation, would have lashed out or walked away. But he chose patience, trusting that time would reveal the wisdom of restraint.

In the months that followed, the truce held. People traveled freely between the two cities, and old enemies began to see each other as human again. The message of Islam spread more quickly than ever, carried not by force, but by the example of those who lived it. The community in Medina grew, not just in numbers, but in confidence. They had learned that true strength was not measured by victory in battle, but by the ability to forgive and to seek reconciliation.

Looking back, it became clear that this period was a turning point. The believers had moved from a posture of constant defense to one of quiet confidence. They no longer saw themselves as victims, but as stewards of a message that could heal old wounds and build new bridges. The lessons of patience, humility, and hope became the foundation for everything that followed.

## Chapter 7 The Final Test—Conquest and Compassion

As the years passed, the balance of power in Arabia began to shift. The truce with Mecca, once a fragile hope, was broken by an act of betrayal. Tensions flared, and the possibility of open conflict loomed once more. But this time, the believers were not the small, vulnerable group they had once been. Their numbers had grown, and their reputation for justice and mercy had spread far and wide.

When the time came to march on Mecca, the decision weighed heavily on Muhammad. He knew the city’s streets and alleys, its people and their fears. He remembered the pain of exile, the sting of rejection. But he also knew that the cycle of violence had to end. The army that set out from Medina was large, but its purpose was not conquest for the sake of power. It was a journey toward reconciliation.

As they approached Mecca, the city braced for the worst. But what happened next surprised everyone. Instead of revenge, Muhammad offered amnesty. He entered the city with humility, his head bowed in gratitude. The people who had once driven him out now saw a leader who chose mercy over vengeance. Old enemies were forgiven, and the idols that had stood for generations were removed from the Ka’bah, restoring it to its original purpose as a place of worship for the one God.

For many, this was a moment of profound transformation. The city that had been the heart of opposition now became a center of faith. People who had once feared change now found themselves drawn to the message of compassion and justice. The bonds of kinship, once twisted by rivalry, were renewed in the spirit of forgiveness.

It became clear that true victory was not in the defeat of an enemy, but in the healing of a community. Most people, when given the chance to settle old scores, would have taken it. But Muhammad’s example showed that the greatest strength lies in letting go of the past and building a future together.

The conquest of Mecca was not the end of the journey, but the beginning of a new chapter. The lessons of mercy, humility, and reconciliation became the guiding principles for the growing community. The world watched as former adversaries stood side by side, united by a vision of justice and peace.

## Chapter 8 The Farewell Pilgrimage—A Legacy of Guidance

With Mecca now a place of peace, the message of Islam spread rapidly across Arabia. Tribes that had once been divided by old grudges and rivalries now found common ground in a shared faith. The community in Medina, once a small band of outcasts, had become a beacon of hope for people from every corner of the land.

In the final years of his life, Muhammad’s focus shifted from conflict to consolidation. He worked tirelessly to ensure that the principles of justice, compassion, and equality were woven into the fabric of daily life. He listened to the concerns of the vulnerable, resolved disputes with fairness, and reminded people that true faith was measured by kindness to others.

One of the most significant moments came when he led his followers on a pilgrimage to Mecca. The journey was both a return to the roots of faith and a celebration of unity. People from every tribe and background gathered together, performing the ancient rites in a spirit of humility and gratitude. It was a scene of remarkable diversity and harmony—a living testament to the power of shared purpose.

During this pilgrimage, Muhammad delivered a message that would echo through the ages. He spoke of the equality of all people, regardless of race or status. He reminded his followers that the bonds of faith were stronger than the divisions of tribe or lineage. He called for the protection of women, the care of the vulnerable, and the pursuit of justice in all things.

For many, these words were a revelation. They saw that the true legacy of Muhammad was not just in the rituals or the laws, but in the spirit of compassion and dignity that he embodied. Most people, when given authority, are tempted to use it for their own gain. But he used his influence to lift others up, to remind them of their worth, and to call them to something greater than themselves.

The farewell pilgrimage was a moment of both celebration and reflection. The community had come a long way, but the journey was not over. The lessons learned along the way—about patience, forgiveness, and the power of faith—would guide them through whatever challenges lay ahead.

## Chapter 9 The Last Days—Strength in Vulnerability

As the seasons changed and the years passed, the pace of life in Medina slowed. The community had grown, and the message of Islam had reached every corner of Arabia. Yet, for those closest to Muhammad, there was a sense that a chapter was drawing to a close.

In his final days, Muhammad’s strength began to wane. The man who had once led armies and counseled kings now spent more time in quiet reflection. He continued to lead prayers, to offer guidance, and to care for those in need. But there was a gentleness in his presence—a sense of peace that came from a life lived with purpose.

Those around him watched with a mixture of sorrow and gratitude. They remembered the hardships they had faced together, the moments of fear and hope, the lessons learned in the crucible of adversity. They saw in him not just a leader, but a friend, a mentor, and a source of comfort.

In his final illness, Muhammad’s concern was not for himself, but for his community. He reminded his followers to care for one another, to uphold the values that had brought them together, and to remain steadfast in their faith. He asked for forgiveness from those he might have wronged, and he forgave those who had wronged him.

When the end came, it was marked by both grief and gratitude. The loss was profound, but the sense of purpose remained. The community gathered, supporting one another through their sorrow, and finding strength in the memory of the man who had guided them through so much.

Looking back, it became clear that the true measure of Muhammad’s legacy was not in the battles won or the cities conquered, but in the hearts he had touched. Most people, when faced with their own mortality, are consumed by regret or fear. But he faced the end with dignity, humility, and a deep trust in the goodness of those he left behind.

## Chapter 10 The Enduring Legacy—A Light for Generations

After Muhammad’s passing, the world felt both emptier and more full of possibility. The community he had built was left with the task of carrying his message forward, of living out the values he had taught by example. The challenges were real—old rivalries resurfaced, and the temptation to return to familiar patterns was strong. But the memory of his compassion, his patience, and his unwavering sense of justice served as a guiding light.

People remembered the way he had listened to the concerns of the weakest among them, the way he had forgiven those who had wronged him, and the way he had always chosen mercy over anger. They recalled the moments of laughter and sorrow, the quiet acts of kindness, and the courage to speak the truth even when it was difficult.

The lessons of his life became stories passed from parent to child, from teacher to student. They were not just tales of the past, but living reminders of what was possible when people chose hope over fear, and compassion over division. The community continued to grow, drawing strength from the example set by their beloved leader.

Over time, the message of Islam spread far beyond the borders of Arabia. People from every background found meaning in its call to justice, mercy, and the dignity of every human being. The world changed, but the core values remained the same—a commitment to truth, to kindness, and to the belief that every person has a role to play in building a better world.

Looking back, it became clear that the true miracle was not in the events themselves, but in the transformation of hearts and minds. Most people, when faced with adversity, are tempted to give up or to turn inward. But the story of Muhammad and his community showed that even in the darkest moments, there is always a path forward—a way to turn pain into purpose, and struggle into strength.

## Conclusion

The story of Muhammad, as told in *The Sealed Nectar*, is not just a chronicle of events, but a living guide for anyone seeking meaning, resilience, and hope in the face of life’s challenges. His journey—from orphaned child to trusted leader, from exile to reconciler—offers a blueprint for navigating hardship with grace and for building community on the foundation of compassion and justice.

- True leadership is rooted in humility, patience, and service to others.
- Forgiveness and reconciliation are more powerful than revenge or resentment.
- Community thrives when built on trust, empathy, and shared purpose.
- Adversity can deepen character and reveal hidden strengths.
- The legacy of compassion endures far beyond any single lifetime.

If you find yourself facing uncertainty, remember the quiet strength that comes from holding fast to your values, even when the world seems against you. Let the example of Muhammad remind you that every act of kindness, every moment of patience, and every choice to forgive can ripple outward, shaping not just your own life, but the lives of those around you. The journey is never easy, and the path is often unclear, but the lessons of the past offer reassurance: with faith, courage, and compassion, it is always possible to build something better—one day, one choice, and one heart at a time.
