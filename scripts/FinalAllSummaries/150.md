Shakespeare drew on this shared knowledge, using plants to create a kind of shorthand that his audience would recognize.

But the meanings of plants weren’t fixed. They could change depending on the context, the character, or the mood of the scene. A rose might mean love in one play and secrecy in another. This flexibility allowed Shakespeare to play with expectations, surprising his audience and deepening the emotional impact of his words.

But the relationship between people and plants wasn’t just practical—it was also emotional and spiritual. Many believed that certain plants had the power to protect against evil or bring good luck.
