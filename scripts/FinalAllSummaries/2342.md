## Introduction

The Pleasures of God by John Piper invites reflection on the profound and often overlooked reality of divine joy. At the heart of existence lies a God who delights in His own glory, whose happiness is not detached or impersonal, but deeply woven into the fabric of creation. The nature of God’s pleasure—what brings Him joy, what He values, and how He expresses delight—shapes the universe and the lives within it. This exploration moves beyond duty and obligation, drawing attention to the beauty and satisfaction found in God’s own enjoyment of being God, and how that joy overflows into the world.

As the meditations unfold, the focus turns to the ways God’s delight influences everything from His love for His Son to His acts of mercy, justice, and grace. The vision expands, revealing how understanding God’s pleasures transforms the way people see worship, obedience, and the pursuit of happiness. The journey through these themes offers a fresh perspective on the relationship between divine joy and human fulfillment, inviting a deeper encounter with the source of all true pleasure.

---

## Chapter 1 Discovering What Brings God Joy

There’s a moment, often quiet and unremarkable, when someone sits back and wonders what truly makes another person happy. It might happen in a kitchen, with a parent watching their child laugh, or in a park, as friends share a joke. The question lingers: what brings real delight? Now, imagine turning that question toward God. Not just what pleases people, but what stirs joy in the heart of the One who made everything. This is where the journey begins—not with a list of rules or duties, but with a longing to know what makes God glad.

Most people, when they think about God, picture a distant ruler or a judge keeping score. But the story of God’s joy is different. It’s about a God who is not only powerful and wise, but also deeply happy—delighting in Himself, in His Son, and in all that He does. This isn’t a cold, abstract idea. It’s the heartbeat of the universe, the source of every true and lasting joy.

For many, the search for happiness feels endless. There’s the young professional, exhausted after another long week, wondering if the next promotion will finally bring contentment. Or the parent, quietly hoping that their child’s success will fill the emptiness inside. Even in moments of celebration, a quiet ache remains. The world offers countless ways to chase happiness, but few ever find it for long.

What if the answer isn’t found in chasing after our own pleasures, but in discovering what brings pleasure to God? This question changes everything. It shifts the focus from self to something far greater. It invites people to look beyond their own desires and to see the world through God’s eyes.

The story of God’s joy begins with Himself. Before anything existed—before stars, before people, before time—God was already full of delight. He didn’t create the world out of loneliness or need. He was already complete, already overflowing with happiness. This is hard to grasp, especially in a world where happiness often feels fragile and fleeting. But for God, joy is not something He finds outside Himself. It is who He is.

This truth has a way of unsettling old assumptions. Many grow up thinking that God’s main concern is with human behavior—rewarding the good, punishing the bad. But the deeper story is about God’s own gladness. He delights in His own glory, in the beauty and perfection of His character. This isn’t selfishness; it’s the foundation of all that is good and beautiful. If God were to delight in anything less than Himself, He would be settling for something unworthy.

There’s a quiet freedom in realizing that God’s happiness doesn’t depend on people. He is not needy or insecure. His joy is unshakable, rooted in His own greatness. This means that the invitation to know God is not a burden, but a gift. It’s an invitation to share in the joy that has always existed, long before anyone was born.

As the story unfolds, it becomes clear that understanding what pleases God is not just a theological exercise. It’s the key to true worship, to lasting happiness, and to a life that makes sense. When people see God as He truly is—full of joy, delighting in Himself and His works—their own hearts begin to change. Worship becomes more than a duty; it becomes a response to the beauty and gladness of God.

## Chapter 2 God’s Joy in His Own Glory

Imagine a craftsman, alone in his workshop, shaping a piece of wood with careful hands. He steps back, not to show off, but to admire the beauty of what he’s made. There’s a quiet satisfaction in the work itself, a pleasure that comes from seeing something good and true. In a much deeper way, God’s joy is rooted in the beauty and perfection of His own character—His glory.

For many, the word “glory” feels distant, even intimidating. It conjures images of bright lights, grand ceremonies, or unreachable standards. But at its heart, glory is about worth and beauty. It’s what makes something—or someone—truly valuable. When God delights in His own glory, He is rejoicing in the fullness of who He is: perfect, wise, loving, and just.

This idea can feel strange at first. People are taught to avoid pride and self-centeredness. But for God, delighting in Himself is not arrogance; it’s the only fitting response to infinite goodness. If God were to value anything above Himself, He would be denying the truth of His own perfection. His joy in His glory is the foundation of all that is right and beautiful.

There’s a story that plays out in countless lives—a student striving for approval, an artist hoping for recognition, a friend longing to be noticed. The desire for glory, for worth, is woven into every heart. But when people seek it in themselves or in others, it always falls short. The glory fades, the applause dies down, and the emptiness returns.

God’s glory, on the other hand, never fades. It is unchanging, eternal, and utterly satisfying. When people catch even a glimpse of it, their own desires are transformed. The need for approval, the hunger for recognition, begins to lose its grip. Instead, there is a growing longing to see and savor the beauty of God.

This is where worship finds its true meaning. It’s not about performing rituals or following rules. It’s about responding to the worth of God with awe and delight. When people see God’s glory, they can’t help but rejoice. Their hearts are drawn out of themselves and into the wonder of something far greater.

But this joy is not just for God alone. He invites people to share in it, to find their own happiness in His happiness. This is the secret that many miss: true joy is found not in seeking one’s own glory, but in delighting in the glory of God. When people make much of Him, their own hearts are filled with a joy that nothing else can match.

There’s a quiet revolution that happens when this truth takes hold. The endless striving for approval begins to fade. The need to prove oneself loses its power. Instead, there is a growing freedom—a sense that life is not about making much of oneself, but about enjoying the One who is truly worthy.

## Chapter 3 The Father’s Delight in the Son

Picture a father watching his child take their first steps. There’s a mixture of pride, love, and sheer delight in his eyes. He isn’t just pleased with the achievement; he’s overjoyed by the child’s very being. In a far deeper and more perfect way, God the Father finds His greatest joy in His Son, Jesus Christ.

This relationship is at the heart of everything. Before the world began, before a single star was set in the sky, the Father and the Son shared a love and joy that was complete and overflowing. The Son is the perfect image of the Father’s glory—the exact expression of His character and beauty. When the Father looks at the Son, He sees all that is good and true about Himself reflected back in perfect harmony.

For many, the idea of God as Father can be difficult. Some carry memories of disappointment or absence, of fathers who were distant or demanding. But the relationship between the Father and the Son is different. It is marked by perfect love, mutual delight, and unbroken fellowship. This is not a cold, formal relationship, but a living, joyful bond.

There’s a story that echoes in many families—a parent who finds joy not just in what their child does, but in who they are. The laughter at the dinner table, the quiet moments of understanding, the simple presence of a loved one—these are the things that bring real delight. In the same way, the Father’s pleasure in the Son is not based on performance or achievement. It is rooted in the very being of the Son, who perfectly reflects the Father’s heart.

This truth has profound implications. The joy that the Father finds in the Son is the same joy that He invites people to share. When someone comes to know Jesus, they are drawn into the circle of this divine delight. They are welcomed, not as outsiders or servants, but as beloved children. The love that has always existed between the Father and the Son becomes the foundation of their own relationship with God.

Many spend their lives trying to earn approval, to prove their worth. But in the story of the Father and the Son, a different message emerges. Approval is not something to be achieved, but a gift to be received. The Father’s delight in the Son is unchanging, and those who are united to the Son by faith are welcomed into that same delight.

This changes the way people see themselves and others. No longer defined by success or failure, by the opinions of others or the standards of the world, they find their identity in the love of the Father. Worship becomes a celebration of this love—a joyful response to the beauty and worth of the Son.

## Chapter 4 God’s Joy in Creation and His Works

There’s a certain wonder that fills the air on a clear morning, when sunlight spills across the fields and birdsong drifts through open windows. Children run barefoot in the grass, marveling at the world around them. In these moments, it’s easy to sense that creation itself is a gift—something meant to be enjoyed, not just used.

God’s joy in creation is woven into the fabric of the world. When He made the heavens and the earth, He didn’t do it out of boredom or necessity. He created because it pleased Him, because the beauty and order of the universe reflected His own glory. Every mountain, every river, every living thing is a testimony to the delight of the Creator.

Most people, caught up in the rush of daily life, miss this. They see the world as a backdrop for their own stories, a resource to be managed or a problem to be solved. But when the pace slows and the noise fades, the wonder returns. The intricate patterns of a leaf, the vastness of the night sky, the laughter of a child—these are reminders that creation is not an accident, but a work of art.

There’s a story that unfolds in every garden, every forest, every city park—a story of beauty, order, and purpose. The world is not random or meaningless. It is the handiwork of a God who delights in what He has made. This delight is not distant or abstract. It is present in every sunrise, every changing season, every act of kindness.

But God’s pleasure in creation goes beyond the physical world. He also delights in His works of justice, mercy, and redemption. When wrongs are made right, when the oppressed are set free, when broken lives are restored, God rejoices. His joy is not limited to the beauty of nature; it extends to the beauty of lives transformed by His grace.

Many struggle to see this. The world is full of pain and injustice, of suffering and loss. It’s easy to wonder where God’s joy fits into such a broken reality. But the story of creation is also a story of hope. God is not indifferent to suffering. He is at work, bringing light into darkness, healing into brokenness, and joy into sorrow.

There’s a quiet confidence that comes from knowing this. Even in the midst of chaos, there is a sense that the world is not out of control. The Creator is still at work, still delighting in His creation, still bringing about His purposes. This changes the way people see the world. Every act of beauty, every moment of justice, every step toward healing becomes a sign of God’s ongoing pleasure in His works.

## Chapter 5 God’s Pleasure in Justice, Love, and Redemption

In every community, there are moments when someone stands up for what is right—a neighbor who speaks out against injustice, a friend who forgives a deep hurt, a stranger who offers help without expecting anything in return. These moments, though often small and unnoticed, reflect something of God’s own heart. They are glimpses of the justice, love, and redemption that bring Him joy.

God’s pleasure in justice is not a cold, legalistic demand for order. It is the delight of a Father who longs to see His children treated with dignity and respect. When the weak are protected, when the oppressed are defended, when wrongs are made right, God rejoices. His justice is not about punishment for its own sake, but about restoring what is broken and making things whole.

There’s a story that plays out in every act of forgiveness—a relationship strained by betrayal, a wound that seems too deep to heal. Yet, when forgiveness is offered, something miraculous happens. The weight of anger lifts, and the possibility of reconciliation emerges. In these moments, God’s pleasure in love becomes real. He delights in mercy, in compassion, in the restoration of broken relationships.

But God’s joy in love goes even deeper. It is most clearly seen in His work of redemption—the rescue of people from sin and death. This is not a distant, impersonal transaction. It is the passionate pursuit of a God who refuses to let His creation be lost. When someone turns from darkness to light, when a life is transformed by grace, God’s heart is filled with joy.

Many struggle to believe this. They carry the weight of guilt, the fear of rejection, the sense that they are beyond hope. But the story of redemption is a story of relentless love. God’s pleasure is not in condemning, but in saving. He delights in showing mercy, in welcoming the lost, in making all things new.

This changes the way people see themselves and others. No one is beyond the reach of God’s love. No failure is final, no wound too deep. The invitation is always open—to receive forgiveness, to extend mercy, to join in the work of justice and restoration.

Worship, in this light, becomes more than singing songs or saying prayers. It is a way of life—a response to the love and justice of God. It is the choice to join in His joy, to delight in what delights Him, to become agents of His redemption in the world.

## Chapter 6 God’s Delight in His People

There’s a quiet moment that happens sometimes at the end of a long day—a parent watching their child sleep, a teacher seeing a student finally understand, a friend noticing the small, unnoticed kindnesses of another. In these moments, a deep affection wells up, not because of perfection, but because of love. This is a small echo of the way God delights in His people.

For many, it’s hard to imagine being a source of joy for God. The world is full of reminders of failure, of not measuring up, of being overlooked. Yet, the story of God’s pleasure in His people is woven throughout history. It’s not about flawless performance or constant success. It’s about belonging, about being chosen and cherished.

There’s a scene that plays out in the lives of those who feel unseen—a person who quietly serves, who forgives when it’s hard, who keeps hoping when hope seems foolish. Most never notice, but God does. His delight is not reserved for the impressive or the powerful. He finds joy in the humble, the faithful, the ones who trust Him even when it’s costly.

It became clear, as the story unfolds, that God’s pleasure in His people is rooted in His own love, not in their achievements. He rejoices over them with singing, the way a parent might hum a lullaby over a beloved child. This isn’t sentimentality; it’s a deep, unwavering commitment. Even when His people wander, even when they fail, His love remains.

Many fall into the habit of thinking that God’s approval must be earned, that His delight is fragile or conditional. But the truth is more surprising. God’s joy in His people is anchored in His promises, in the work of His Son, and in the transforming power of His Spirit. He sees not just what is, but what will be—the beauty that is being formed, the story that is still unfolding.

This changes everything. The search for worth, the fear of rejection, the pressure to perform—all begin to lose their grip. Instead, there is a growing sense of security, a quiet confidence that comes from being loved by the One who knows everything and still delights. Worship, then, becomes a celebration of this love—a way of saying thank you, of resting in the joy that God takes in His people.

## Chapter 7 The Joy of God in Obedience and Faith

There’s a certain satisfaction that comes from doing what is right, even when no one is watching. A worker who finishes a task with care, a friend who keeps a promise, a neighbor who chooses honesty over convenience—these moments carry a quiet dignity. In a deeper way, God finds joy in the obedience and faith of His people.

Obedience, for many, is a word that brings to mind rules and restrictions. It can feel like a burden, a list of things to do or avoid. But in the story of God’s pleasure, obedience is something different. It’s not about earning approval or avoiding punishment. It’s about trust, about responding to love with love.

There’s a story that unfolds in the life of someone who chooses the harder path—a person who forgives when it would be easier to hold a grudge, who gives generously when resources are tight, who speaks truth when silence would be safer. These choices are often unseen, sometimes misunderstood. But they matter. They are acts of faith, small seeds of trust planted in the soil of daily life.

Most people don’t notice this, but God does. His joy is not in perfection, but in the heart that turns toward Him, again and again. Obedience becomes a way of saying, “I trust You. I believe Your way is good.” It’s not about getting everything right, but about walking in relationship, about listening and responding.

Faith, too, is a source of God’s delight. It’s the quiet confidence that He is who He says He is, even when circumstances are hard. It’s the willingness to step out, to risk, to hope. There’s a kind of beauty in faith that can’t be measured by success or results. It’s the beauty of trust, of leaning into the unknown with the assurance that God is faithful.

This changes the way people see obedience and faith. They are not burdens to bear, but gifts to give. They are ways of sharing in God’s joy, of participating in the story He is writing. Worship, then, becomes more than words or rituals. It becomes a life lived in response to love—a daily offering of trust and obedience.

## Chapter 8 God’s Joy in Prayer and Communion

There’s a special kind of closeness that happens in honest conversation—a late-night talk between friends, a letter written from the heart, a quiet moment of listening. In these moments, barriers fall away, and real connection is possible. In a similar way, God finds joy in the prayers and communion of His people.

For many, prayer feels like a duty, a box to check, or a last resort when things go wrong. But the story of God’s pleasure in prayer is different. It’s about relationship, about being known and heard. God is not distant or disinterested. He delights in the voices of His people, in their questions, their hopes, their fears.

There’s a scene that plays out in countless lives—a person sitting alone, unsure of what to say, yet choosing to speak anyway. The words may be halting, the thoughts scattered, but the act itself is precious. God listens, not for eloquence, but for honesty. He welcomes the stammering, the silence, the sighs too deep for words.

It became clear that prayer is not about getting everything right, but about drawing near. It’s an invitation to share life with God, to bring joys and sorrows, dreams and disappointments. In these moments of communion, something shifts. The burdens feel lighter, the loneliness less sharp, the hope more real.

Many fall into the habit of thinking that God is too busy, too far away, or too unconcerned to care. But the truth is more tender. God’s heart is moved by the prayers of His people. He delights in their trust, in their willingness to come close. Prayer is not a transaction; it’s a relationship.

This changes the way people approach God. No longer is prayer a performance or a last resort. It becomes a way of life—a rhythm of conversation, a habit of drawing near. Worship, then, is not just what happens in a building or on a special day. It’s the ongoing communion of hearts, the daily sharing of life with the One who listens and delights.

## Chapter 9 The Joy of God in Suffering and Sacrifice

There’s a kind of beauty that only emerges in the hardest places—a nurse holding a trembling hand, a friend sitting quietly beside a hospital bed, a family choosing hope in the face of loss. These moments are marked by pain, but also by a strange, unexpected joy. In a mysterious way, God finds pleasure even in suffering and sacrifice.

For many, this is the hardest part of the story to accept. Suffering feels pointless, unfair, even cruel. It’s easy to wonder how any good could come from pain. Yet, the story of God’s pleasure is not one of denial or escape. It’s a story of presence, of purpose, of hope that endures.

There’s a scene that unfolds in the life of someone facing loss—a person who chooses to forgive, to love, to trust, even when it hurts. These choices are not easy. They come with tears, with questions, with moments of doubt. But they are also moments of deep connection, of grace that cannot be explained.

Most people don’t notice this, but God does. His joy is not in the suffering itself, but in the love that endures, the faith that persists, the hope that refuses to die. Suffering becomes a place where God’s presence is most real, where His comfort is most needed, where His strength is most evident.

It became clear that sacrifice is not about loss for its own sake, but about love. When someone gives up comfort, security, or even dreams for the sake of another, something beautiful happens. God delights in these acts of self-giving, in the willingness to lay down one’s life for others.

This changes the way people see suffering and sacrifice. They are not meaningless or wasted. They are places where God’s joy is revealed in unexpected ways. Worship, then, becomes an act of trust—a declaration that God is good, even when life is hard.

## Chapter 10 The Pleasures of God and the Life of Worship

There’s a moment, sometimes quiet and sometimes full of music, when a group of people gather to sing, to pray, to listen. In these moments, something shifts. The focus moves from the worries of the week to the wonder of something greater. This is the heart of worship—a response to the pleasures of God.

For many, worship is a routine, a set of songs or rituals. But the story of God’s pleasure transforms worship into something deeper. It becomes a celebration, a feast, a joyful response to the beauty and goodness of God. Worship is not about impressing God or earning His favor. It’s about delighting in what delights Him.

There’s a scene that plays out in every gathering—a person who comes weary, another who comes grateful, another who comes searching. Each brings their own story, their own hopes and fears. Yet, as voices rise together, as prayers are spoken, as hearts are lifted, something happens. The pleasures of God become the anchor, the center, the source of joy.

It became clear that worship is not just for special occasions. It’s a way of life—a daily turning toward God, a habit of noticing, of giving thanks, of rejoicing in what is good. Worship is found in the ordinary moments—the laughter of friends, the beauty of creation, the quiet acts of kindness.

Many fall into the habit of thinking that worship is about performance or perfection. But the truth is more freeing. Worship is about presence, about being with God, about sharing in His joy. It’s about letting go of self and entering into the wonder of who God is.

This changes everything. The pressures of achievement, the fears of failure, the weight of expectation—all begin to fade. In their place is a growing sense of freedom, of gratitude, of joy. Worship becomes a way of living, a way of seeing, a way of loving.

## Chapter 11 The Pleasures of God and Everyday Life

There’s a gentle rhythm to daily life—the morning routines, the work that fills the hours, the meals shared with family or friends. In these ordinary moments, it’s easy to forget that anything sacred is happening. Yet, the story of God’s pleasure invites people to see everyday life in a new way.

For many, faith feels like something reserved for special times or places. But the truth is that God’s joy is present in the ordinary, the routine, the unnoticed. Every act of kindness, every word of encouragement, every moment of patience is a chance to share in the pleasures of God.

There’s a scene that unfolds in the life of someone who chooses to love in small ways—a parent who listens, a coworker who helps, a neighbor who cares. These acts may seem insignificant, but they are precious. God delights in the ordinary faithfulness of His people, in the quiet ways they reflect His love.

It became clear that the pleasures of God are not just for the extraordinary or the heroic. They are for everyone, everywhere, every day. The invitation is to live with eyes open, to notice the beauty, to celebrate the good, to join in the work of love and justice.

Many fall into the habit of thinking that their lives don’t matter, that their efforts are too small to count. But the truth is more hopeful. Every moment is an opportunity to share in God’s joy, to bring light into darkness, to make a difference.

This changes the way people see their work, their relationships, their daily routines. Everything becomes a chance to worship, to love, to rejoice. The pleasures of God become the foundation of a life that is both meaningful and full of wonder.

## Chapter 12 The Pleasures of God and the Hope of Glory

There’s a longing that runs through every heart—a hope for something more, a dream of a world made right. It shows up in the stories people tell, in the songs they sing, in the prayers they whisper in the dark. This longing is not a mistake. It’s a signpost, pointing to the hope of glory.

For many, the world feels broken, full of disappointment and loss. The pleasures of God can seem distant, even impossible. But the story is not over. The hope of glory is the promise that one day, all things will be made new. The joy that God has always known will fill the world, and every longing will be satisfied.

There’s a scene that plays out in the lives of those who wait—a person who keeps hoping, who keeps loving, who keeps believing, even when the evidence is thin. This hope is not wishful thinking. It’s rooted in the character of God, in the certainty of His promises, in the reality of His joy.

It became clear that the pleasures of God are not just for this life. They are the foundation of a hope that endures, a joy that cannot be taken away. The promise is that, one day, every tear will be wiped away, every sorrow will be turned to joy, every broken thing will be made whole.

Many fall into the habit of despair, of cynicism, of giving up. But the truth is more powerful. The hope of glory is the assurance that the story ends in joy, that the pleasures of God will fill the earth, that every longing will be met.

This changes the way people live now. Hope becomes a source of strength, a reason to keep going, a light in the darkness. The pleasures of God become the anchor, the promise, the song that carries people through every season.

## Conclusion: Living in the Light of God’s Joy

If you’ve walked through these pages, you’ve seen that the pleasures of God are not distant or abstract. They are the heartbeat of reality, the source of every true and lasting joy. To know what brings God delight is to find the secret of a life that is both meaningful and full of wonder.

**Key Takeaways:**
- God’s joy is rooted in His own glory, not in human achievement.
- The Father’s delight in the Son is the foundation of all true love.
- God finds pleasure in creation, justice, mercy, and the transformation of lives.
- Obedience, faith, and prayer are not burdens, but invitations to share in God’s joy.
- Every ordinary moment is a chance to reflect and participate in the pleasures of God.

As you move forward, remember that you are invited into this joy—not as a spectator, but as a participant. The story of God’s pleasure is not just something to admire from a distance. It’s a call to live differently, to see the world with new eyes, to find meaning in the ordinary and hope in the hardest places. You don’t have to earn God’s delight; you are welcomed into it. Let this truth shape your worship, your work, your relationships, and your dreams. When you live in the light of God’s joy, you become part of a story that is bigger, deeper, and more beautiful than you ever imagined. And in that story, you will find a happiness that endures—a joy that is both your own and God’s, forever intertwined.
