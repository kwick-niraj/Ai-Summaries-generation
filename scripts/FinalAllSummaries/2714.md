## Introduction

Questions of faith, morality, and the nature of good and evil have long shaped the human experience, inviting both skepticism and wonder. In the world of C.S. Lewis’s Mere Christianity and The Screwtape Letters, the search for meaning unfolds through a blend of reasoned argument and imaginative exploration. The journey begins with the recognition of a universal moral law, a sense of right and wrong that transcends culture and time, hinting at something greater than mere social convention. This moral compass, Lewis suggests, points toward the existence of a higher power, inviting reflection on the foundations of belief, the challenges of doubt, and the transformative potential of grace.

As the exploration deepens, the struggle between virtue and vice takes center stage, not as an abstract battle but as a lived reality within every heart. Through the clever satire of demonic correspondence and the clear-eyed logic of Christian apologetics, the complexities of temptation, pride, forgiveness, and love are laid bare. The interplay between rational inquiry and spiritual insight offers a compelling vision of what it means to live with integrity, courage, and hope. The path is neither simple nor free from struggle, but it is illuminated by the promise that even in the midst of confusion and conflict, the pursuit of truth and goodness remains both possible and profoundly worthwhile.
