## Introduction

Life often presents a fork in the road, where the well-trodden path of obligation—what one "should" do—meets the less certain, but deeply compelling, route of personal calling—what one "must" do. The Crossroads of Should and Must: Find and Follow Your Passion by Elle Luna explores this universal tension, inviting a closer look at the forces that shape choices and the courage required to honor authentic desires. The world of "should" is filled with expectations, social norms, and the comfort of predictability, while "must" beckons with the promise of fulfillment, creativity, and the risk of stepping into the unknown. Navigating this intersection becomes a journey of self-discovery, where the stakes are nothing less than a life lived in alignment with true purpose.

As the journey unfolds, the landscape of "should" and "must" reveals itself through stories, reflections, and practical insights that illuminate the process of discerning one’s own path. The pull of passion and the weight of duty are examined not as opposites, but as forces that can coexist and inform each other. Elle Luna’s perspective encourages a gentle, yet persistent, inquiry into what it means to listen to the inner voice that calls for more—more meaning, more creativity, more authenticity. The crossroads becomes a place of possibility, where the act of choosing "must" is not a single decision, but an ongoing practice of aligning daily actions with the deepest sense of self. From this vantage point, the journey toward passion is both a challenge and an invitation, setting the stage for a life shaped by intention and wholehearted engagement.

## Chapter 1 The Moment of Choice: Feeling Pulled Between Obligation and Desire

There’s a quiet tension that builds in the background of many lives—a sense that something isn’t quite right, even when everything looks fine on the surface. It often starts as a subtle restlessness, a nagging feeling that the path being followed isn’t truly one’s own. For some, it’s the weight of a job that pays the bills but leaves the heart untouched. For others, it’s the pressure to meet family expectations, to fit into a mold shaped by tradition or practicality. This is the crossroads where two powerful forces meet: the world of “Should” and the call of “Must.”

Imagine a person sitting at a desk late at night, surrounded by the trappings of success—a steady paycheck, a tidy apartment, a calendar full of obligations. Yet, beneath the surface, there’s a longing for something more. Maybe it’s the urge to paint, to write, to build, or to travel. The feeling is both exhilarating and terrifying. The mind races with questions: What if this is a mistake? What if everything falls apart? But the heart whispers, What if this is the beginning of something real?

This crossroads is not a one-time event. It’s a recurring moment, a choice that presents itself again and again. The world of “Should” is familiar and safe. It’s the voice that says, “This is what people expect,” or “This is the responsible thing to do.” It’s shaped by parents, teachers, bosses, and even friends—well-meaning voices that want the best, but often define “best” in terms of security and approval.

On the other side is “Must”—the deep, personal calling that feels both urgent and essential. It’s the part of the self that knows what brings joy, what sparks curiosity, what feels meaningful. “Must” is not always practical or easy to explain. It doesn’t come with guarantees. But it’s the source of true fulfillment.

One story that captures this tension involves a woman who spent years climbing the corporate ladder. She was praised for her work, given promotions, and admired by her peers. Yet, every morning, she felt a growing sense of dread. She remembered how, as a child, she would lose herself in drawing for hours, completely absorbed in the act of creation. That memory became a lifeline—a reminder that there was another way to live.

The decision to leave her job didn’t happen overnight. It was a slow process, filled with doubt and second-guessing. She started by setting aside a small corner of her apartment for art supplies. She painted in the evenings, at first just for herself. Gradually, the act of creating became less of a guilty pleasure and more of a necessity. The more she honored this part of herself, the clearer it became that “Must” was not a luxury—it was a calling.

Most people don’t notice how much of their lives are shaped by “Should.” It’s woven into daily routines, career choices, even relationships. The pressure to conform can be subtle—a raised eyebrow, a passing comment, a story about someone who took a risk and failed. Over time, these messages accumulate, making it harder to hear the voice of “Must.”

But the crossroads is always there, waiting. It’s not about making a single, dramatic leap. It’s about recognizing the moment of choice, and being honest about what’s at stake. The first step is simply to notice the difference between what feels like an obligation and what feels like a calling. That awareness is the beginning of change.

## Chapter 2 Understanding “Should”: The Power of Expectations and the Cost of Conformity

The world of “Should” is built on a foundation of expectations—some spoken, others implied. It’s the invisible script that guides decisions, often without conscious awareness. “Should” is the reason someone stays in a job that drains them, or agrees to plans they don’t enjoy, or pursues goals that don’t feel like their own. It’s the force that keeps people moving in the same direction, even when their hearts are pulling them elsewhere.

Consider the story of a man who always dreamed of being a musician. As a child, he would spend hours strumming his guitar, lost in the rhythm and melody. But as he grew older, the voices of “Should” grew louder. His parents encouraged him to choose a stable career. Friends joked about starving artists. Teachers praised his academic achievements and suggested he focus on something “practical.” Over time, the guitar gathered dust in the corner, and he found himself working in an office, counting down the hours until the weekend.

The cost of living by “Should” is not always obvious at first. It can show up as a vague dissatisfaction, a sense of being disconnected from one’s own life. Some people describe it as feeling like they’re living someone else’s story. There’s a comfort in following the rules, in meeting expectations, in doing what’s considered “right.” But that comfort often comes at the expense of authenticity.

“Should” is not inherently bad. It can provide structure, stability, and a sense of belonging. It’s the reason people show up on time, pay their bills, and keep their promises. But when “Should” becomes the only guide, it can lead to a life that feels hollow. The danger is not in following expectations, but in losing sight of what truly matters.

One woman found herself at a prestigious university, studying a subject she had little interest in. Her family was proud, her friends were impressed, and her future seemed secure. But inside, she felt numb. She realized that every decision she had made was based on what she thought she was supposed to do. The turning point came when she allowed herself to imagine a different future—one where she pursued her love of writing, even if it meant disappointing others.

Recognizing the influence of “Should” is the first step toward change. It requires honesty and self-reflection. Most people are so accustomed to following the script that they don’t even realize it’s there. But once it’s named, it becomes possible to question it. Is this what I truly want? Or is this what I think I’m supposed to want?

Letting go of “Should” is not about rejecting responsibility or abandoning commitments. It’s about making space for authenticity. It’s about finding the courage to ask difficult questions, and being willing to live with the uncertainty that comes with honest answers. The process is rarely easy, but it’s the only way to create a life that feels truly one’s own.

## Chapter 3 Discovering “Must”: Listening to the Inner Voice and Honoring Passion

While “Should” is shaped by the outside world, “Must” comes from within. It’s the quiet, persistent voice that knows what brings joy, what feels meaningful, what makes time disappear. “Must” is not always loud or obvious. Sometimes it’s a faint whisper, drowned out by the noise of daily life. But it’s always there, waiting to be heard.

There’s a story of someone who spent years working in finance, excelling at every task, earning praise and promotions. On paper, everything looked perfect. But inside, there was a growing sense of emptiness. The turning point came during a vacation, when a chance encounter with a street artist sparked a memory of childhood afternoons spent drawing. The feeling was so strong, it was almost physical—a sense of coming home.

For many, the journey toward “Must” begins with small acts of curiosity. It might be signing up for a pottery class, dusting off an old camera, or spending an afternoon lost in a favorite book. These moments are easy to dismiss as hobbies or distractions. But for those who pay attention, they become clues—signposts pointing toward a deeper calling.

“Must” is not about grand gestures or dramatic changes. It’s about honoring what feels true, even in small ways. One person started waking up an hour earlier each day to write before work. Another set aside a corner of the living room for painting. Over time, these small acts added up, creating a sense of momentum and possibility.

The challenge is that “Must” often comes with uncertainty. There are no guarantees of success, no clear path, no promise of approval. Choosing “Must” can feel risky, even reckless. It requires trust—in oneself, in the process, in the belief that passion is worth pursuing, even if the outcome is unknown.

Many fall into the habit of waiting for permission—to be chosen, to be validated, to be told it’s okay to follow their dreams. But “Must” doesn’t work that way. It’s a personal decision, one that can only be made from within. The world may never offer a clear sign. The only way forward is to listen, to trust, and to act.

One artist described the experience of choosing “Must” as both terrifying and exhilarating. There were moments of doubt, days when nothing seemed to work, times when the old life of “Should” seemed easier. But there was also a sense of aliveness, a feeling of being fully engaged with the world. Over time, the fear faded, replaced by a quiet confidence.

The journey toward “Must” is different for everyone. There’s no map, no formula, no right way to do it. What matters is the willingness to listen, to experiment, to make mistakes, and to keep going. Passion is not found overnight. It’s discovered through action, through trial and error, through the courage to begin.

## Chapter 4 Facing Fear and Resistance: Navigating Doubt on the Path to Authenticity

Choosing “Must” is rarely a smooth or simple process. The moment someone decides to step away from “Should,” a wave of fear and resistance often follows. Doubt creeps in, whispering warnings about failure, rejection, and regret. The mind conjures up worst-case scenarios, replaying every reason why it’s safer to stay put.

One person, after years of working in a stable but unfulfilling job, finally decided to pursue a lifelong dream of opening a bakery. The excitement was quickly overshadowed by anxiety. What if no one comes? What if the money runs out? What if this is a huge mistake? The fear was so intense, it almost stopped the dream before it began.

Most people underestimate the power of resistance. It shows up in countless ways—procrastination, self-doubt, perfectionism, even physical symptoms like fatigue or illness. Sometimes it’s disguised as practicality: “Now isn’t the right time,” or “Maybe after I save a little more money.” Other times, it takes the form of comparison: “Other people are more talented,” or “It’s too late for me.”

The truth is, fear is a natural part of the process. It’s a sign that something important is at stake. The mind is wired to seek safety, to avoid risk, to protect against the unknown. But growth requires stepping into discomfort, facing uncertainty, and trusting that the journey is worth it.

One way to move through fear is to break the process into small, manageable steps. The person who dreamed of opening a bakery didn’t quit their job overnight. They started by baking for friends, testing recipes, and learning about the business. Each small success built confidence, making the next step a little less daunting.

Another strategy is to reframe failure as part of the process. Most people are so afraid of making mistakes that they never start. But every creative journey is filled with setbacks, false starts, and detours. The key is to see these moments not as evidence of inadequacy, but as opportunities to learn and grow.

Support from others can make a difference. Sharing dreams with trusted friends, mentors, or fellow travelers can provide encouragement and perspective. Sometimes, just knowing that others have faced similar fears is enough to keep going.

The path to “Must” is not about eliminating fear. It’s about moving forward in spite of it. The courage to act, even when the outcome is uncertain, is what separates those who live by “Should” from those who choose “Must.” Over time, the fear loses its power, replaced by a sense of possibility and freedom.

## Chapter 5 Embracing Uncertainty: Building a Life Around What Matters Most

Stepping into the world of “Must” means letting go of certainty. The old markers of success—titles, salaries, approval—no longer provide the same comfort. Instead, there’s a new kind of security, one that comes from within. It’s the confidence that comes from living in alignment with one’s values, from honoring what feels true, from building a life around what matters most.

One person described the experience of leaving a high-powered job to pursue a passion for teaching. The transition was filled with uncertainty. The pay was lower, the hours were longer, and the future was unclear. But there was a sense of purpose that made the challenges worthwhile. Each day brought new opportunities to connect, to inspire, to make a difference.

Most people expect the journey toward “Must” to be linear—a straight path from where they are to where they want to be. In reality, it’s often messy and unpredictable. There are moments of clarity, followed by periods of confusion. There are breakthroughs and setbacks, moments of joy and moments of doubt. The process is more like a spiral than a straight line, with each turn bringing new insights and opportunities.

One way to navigate uncertainty is to focus on small, consistent actions. The person who wanted to write a novel didn’t wait for the perfect idea or the ideal circumstances. They wrote a little each day, even when it felt pointless. Over time, the pages added up, and the story took shape. The act of showing up, day after day, became its own reward.

Another important lesson is to let go of comparison. It’s easy to look at others and feel inadequate, to believe that everyone else has it figured out. But every journey is unique. What works for one person may not work for another. The only way to find fulfillment is to stay true to one’s own path, to trust the process, and to celebrate progress, no matter how small.

Building a life around “Must” is not about achieving a specific goal or reaching a final destination. It’s about creating a way of living that feels authentic, meaningful, and alive. It’s about making choices that reflect who one truly is, rather than who others expect them to be.

The journey is ongoing. There will always be new crossroads, new choices, new opportunities to choose between “Should” and “Must.” The key is to keep listening, to keep experimenting, to keep moving forward. Over time, the world of “Must” becomes less of a distant dream and more of a lived reality.

## Chapter 6 Making Space for “Must”: Creating Room for What Matters

There’s a moment, often quiet and unremarkable, when someone realizes that their days are filled to the brim with obligations, distractions, and noise. The calendar is packed, the phone never stops buzzing, and the to-do list seems endless. In the midst of all this, the voice of “Must” can feel faint, almost impossible to hear. It’s not that the desire to live authentically has faded—it’s just been crowded out.

One evening, a person sat at their kitchen table, surrounded by unopened mail and half-finished projects. They felt a familiar ache—the sense that life was happening elsewhere, that their real self was waiting for a chance to breathe. It became clear that if “Must” was ever going to have a place, something else would have to give.

Most people don’t notice how much of their time is spent on autopilot. There are errands, emails, social obligations, and endless scrolling. The days blur together, and the things that matter most are pushed to the margins. It’s easy to believe that there’s simply no time for passion, no room for creativity, no space for what feels essential.

But the truth is, making space for “Must” is less about finding extra hours and more about making different choices. It starts with small acts of intention—saying no to one commitment, carving out a quiet hour in the morning, turning off notifications for an evening. These moments of stillness create room for the inner voice to be heard.

One person decided to set aside Sunday afternoons for painting. At first, it felt indulgent, even selfish. But over time, that weekly ritual became a lifeline—a sacred space where “Must” could flourish. The act of protecting that time, of treating it as non-negotiable, sent a powerful message: this matters.

It’s not about perfection. There will always be interruptions, setbacks, and days when nothing goes as planned. The key is to keep coming back, to keep making space, even in small ways. Over time, these choices add up, creating a life that feels more spacious, more intentional, more alive.

Most people underestimate the power of small changes. A few minutes of quiet, a single act of creation, a moment of reflection—these are the seeds from which a life of “Must” can grow. The process is ongoing, a daily practice of choosing what matters most.

## Chapter 7 The Art of Saying No: Boundaries as Acts of Self-Respect

For many, the hardest part of choosing “Must” is learning to say no. The world is full of requests, invitations, and expectations. There’s a deep desire to please, to be liked, to avoid conflict. Saying yes feels easier, safer, more polite. But every yes to “Should” is a no to “Must.”

A friend once found themselves overwhelmed by commitments—work projects, social events, family obligations. They felt stretched thin, exhausted, and resentful. The idea of setting boundaries felt impossible. What if people were disappointed? What if relationships suffered? But the cost of always saying yes was becoming too high.

It became clear that boundaries are not walls—they’re bridges to a more authentic life. Saying no is not about rejection or selfishness. It’s an act of self-respect, a way of honoring what truly matters. Most people don’t realize that every time they say yes to something that doesn’t align with their values, they’re saying no to themselves.

The first no is often the hardest. There’s guilt, anxiety, and fear of backlash. But with practice, it gets easier. One person started by declining a single invitation, explaining that they needed time for a personal project. The world didn’t end. In fact, some friends were supportive, even inspired.

Boundaries are not just about protecting time—they’re about protecting energy, focus, and well-being. They create space for “Must” to thrive. Over time, saying no becomes less about what’s being refused and more about what’s being chosen.

It’s a process, not a destination. There will be missteps, moments of overcommitment, and times when old habits resurface. The key is to keep practicing, to keep refining, to keep choosing what feels true. Boundaries are not a sign of weakness—they’re a sign of strength.

## Chapter 8 Sustaining Passion: Weathering the Highs and Lows

The early days of following “Must” are often filled with excitement and possibility. There’s a sense of discovery, a rush of energy, a feeling of being on the right path. But as time goes on, the initial spark can fade. The work becomes routine, obstacles appear, and doubts resurface. Sustaining passion is its own challenge.

A person who started writing every morning found that, after a few months, the words didn’t flow as easily. The novelty had worn off, and the old temptations of “Should” crept back in. It would be easier to sleep in, to check email, to do anything but face the blank page. The excitement of beginning had given way to the grind of persistence.

Most people expect passion to be effortless, a constant source of motivation. But the reality is more complicated. There are days when the work feels tedious, when progress is slow, when inspiration is nowhere to be found. The difference between those who give up and those who keep going is not talent or luck—it’s commitment.

One artist described the process as a series of seasons. There are times of growth and abundance, when everything feels possible. There are also winters—periods of doubt, frustration, and creative drought. The key is to keep showing up, to trust that the cycle will turn, to honor the process even when the results are uncertain.

Sustaining passion requires patience, resilience, and a willingness to embrace the full range of experience. It’s about finding joy in the work itself, not just in the outcome. It’s about celebrating small victories, learning from setbacks, and staying connected to the deeper why.

Most people don’t realize that passion is not a fixed trait—it’s a practice. It’s something that’s built, nurtured, and sustained over time. The journey is rarely easy, but it’s always worth it.

## Chapter 9 Community and Connection: Finding Support on the Journey

No one walks the path of “Must” alone. The journey is filled with challenges, doubts, and moments of loneliness. Having a community—a circle of support, encouragement, and understanding—can make all the difference.

There’s a story of someone who felt isolated in their pursuit of a creative dream. Friends and family didn’t always understand, and the fear of judgment was real. But everything changed when they found a group of like-minded people—others who were also choosing “Must,” in their own ways. The sense of belonging, the shared struggles and triumphs, made the journey feel less daunting.

Most people underestimate the power of connection. Sharing dreams, fears, and setbacks with others creates a sense of accountability and encouragement. It’s easier to keep going when someone else believes in you, when there’s a place to celebrate progress and process disappointment.

Community doesn’t have to be large or formal. It can be a single friend, a mentor, a group of fellow travelers. What matters is the sense of being seen, heard, and supported. These relationships provide perspective, inspiration, and a reminder that the journey is not a solitary one.

One person found that teaching others, sharing their process, and offering encouragement became a source of motivation. Helping others choose “Must” reinforced their own commitment, creating a cycle of support and growth.

The world of “Should” can feel isolating, but the world of “Must” is built on connection. The more people share their stories, the more others are inspired to do the same. Over time, a community of authenticity and courage begins to grow.

## Chapter 10 Integrating “Should” and “Must”: Living Authentically in a Complex World

As the journey unfolds, it becomes clear that “Should” and “Must” are not enemies. They’re two parts of a whole, each with a role to play. The challenge is not to eliminate “Should” or to live entirely in the world of “Must.” It’s to find a balance that feels true, sustainable, and alive.

A parent, for example, may feel the pull of “Must” in the form of creative work, but also the reality of “Should” in the responsibilities of caring for a family. The tension is real, but it doesn’t have to be a source of guilt or frustration. It can be an invitation to integrate, to find ways of honoring both.

Most people imagine that authenticity means abandoning all obligations, but the truth is more nuanced. Living authentically is about making conscious choices, about aligning actions with values, about finding meaning in both “Should” and “Must.” It’s about recognizing that some “Shoulds” are necessary, even valuable, and that “Must” can be woven into the fabric of daily life.

One person found that by bringing their passion for music into their work as a teacher, they were able to honor both worlds. The boundaries between “Should” and “Must” became less rigid, more fluid. The result was a life that felt more integrated, more whole.

The process is ongoing. There will always be new challenges, new choices, new opportunities to recalibrate. The key is to stay awake, to keep listening, to keep choosing what feels true. Over time, the distinction between “Should” and “Must” becomes less about conflict and more about harmony.

## Chapter 11 The Ongoing Journey: Returning to the Crossroads

Even after years of choosing “Must,” the crossroads never fully disappears. Life changes, circumstances shift, and new challenges arise. The voice of “Should” may grow louder at times, and the call of “Must” may fade into the background. The journey is not a one-time event—it’s a lifelong process of returning, again and again, to the moment of choice.

A person who had built a life around their passion found themselves, years later, facing a new set of obligations. The demands of work, family, and aging parents threatened to crowd out the space for “Must.” The old patterns of “Should” reappeared, and the familiar tension returned.

It became clear that the journey is cyclical. There are seasons of expansion and contraction, times of clarity and confusion. The key is not to avoid the crossroads, but to embrace it—to see it as an opportunity for growth, reflection, and renewal.

Most people don’t realize that the process of choosing “Must” is never finished. There will always be new layers, new challenges, new opportunities to deepen the commitment to authenticity. The journey is not about arriving at a final destination, but about learning to navigate the path with courage, grace, and self-compassion.

One person described the process as a dance—a constant movement between obligation and desire, between responsibility and passion. The dance is not always graceful, but it’s always real. The willingness to keep showing up, to keep choosing, to keep listening—that’s what makes the journey meaningful.

## Conclusion: Living at the Crossroads—A Guide for the Journey Ahead

The crossroads of “Should” and “Must” is not a place to visit once and leave behind. It’s a living, breathing part of every life—a space where choices are made, values are tested, and authenticity is forged. The stories and insights from this journey are not just ideas to ponder, but invitations to act.

If you find yourself standing at the crossroads, remember:

- Small, intentional changes can create space for your deepest passions.
- Boundaries are acts of self-respect, not selfishness or rejection.
- Passion is sustained through practice, patience, and showing up daily.
- Community and connection make the journey less lonely and more possible.
- Integration—not perfection—is the goal; balance is a living, ongoing process.

You don’t have to have all the answers. The path is rarely clear, and the journey is never finished. What matters is the willingness to listen—to your own voice, to your own needs, to the quiet call of “Must.” There will be days of doubt, moments of fear, and times when the world of “Should” feels overwhelming. But there will also be moments of clarity, joy, and deep fulfillment.

Give yourself permission to begin, even if the steps are small. Trust that your desires matter, that your voice is worth hearing, and that your life can be shaped by what feels most true. The crossroads will appear again and again. Each time, you have the chance to choose—not just once, but every day. And with each choice, you move closer to a life that is not just lived, but truly your own.
