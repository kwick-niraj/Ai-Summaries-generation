## Chapter 1: Deciding to Learn Everything and Facing the First Pages

The first step was simple in theory: open the first volume and start reading. But the reality was more overwhelming. The encyclopedia, with its dozens of thick volumes, loomed like a mountain. The first entries—abacuses, aardvarks, and the like—were a test of patience and focus. The words were dense, the facts relentless. It was easy to get lost in the details, to wonder if any of this knowledge would ever be useful, or if it was just a way to pass the time.

At home, this new project quickly became a presence. The books took up space, both physically and emotionally. The person’s partner watched with a mix of amusement and concern, unsure whether this was a harmless quirk or the start of an obsession. Friends were skeptical, some supportive, others teasing. The project was met with eye rolls and jokes, but also a quiet admiration for the sheer audacity of it.

As the days passed, the act of reading became a ritual. Each evening, after work, the next few pages would be tackled. Sometimes the facts were fascinating—stories of ancient civilizations, the origins of common words, the strange habits of animals. Other times, the entries blurred together, and it was hard to remember why this had seemed like a good idea. But there was a stubbornness at play, a refusal to give up so soon.

It became clear that this wasn’t just about memorizing facts. It was about testing limits, about seeing how far determination could go. The encyclopedia was a symbol of everything that felt out of reach—wisdom, confidence, a sense of mastery over the world’s chaos. Most people never try to learn everything, and for good reason. But there was a strange joy in the attempt, in the act of pushing against the boundaries of what seemed possible.

The early days were filled with small victories and frustrations. Remembering a fact about a rare insect felt like a triumph, while forgetting a basic historical date was a blow to the ego. The project was already changing the way the world looked—every conversation, every news story, every random encounter became an opportunity to connect a new fact, to see patterns and links that had gone unnoticed before.

But the biggest lesson was humility. No matter how much was read, there was always more. The encyclopedia was endless, and so was the process of learning. The more knowledge was gained, the more gaps appeared. It was both exhilarating and humbling, a reminder that true intelligence isn’t about knowing everything, but about being willing to keep learning, even when the task seems impossible.
