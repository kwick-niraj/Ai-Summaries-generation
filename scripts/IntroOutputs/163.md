**Introduction:**

Behind the public image of Franklin Delano Roosevelt lies a complex interplay of health, secrecy, and power that shaped the course of American history. The presidency of FDR, marked by the Great Depression and World War II, is often remembered for its bold leadership and transformative policies. Yet, beneath the surface, questions linger about the true state of his health and the lengths to which those around him went to conceal his medical realities. The narrative of FDR Unmasked: 73 Years of Medical Cover-ups That Rewrote History by Steven Lomazow delves into the hidden world of presidential illness, exploring how the management of Roosevelt’s health influenced not only his personal decisions but also the fate of a nation during its most critical moments.

As the layers of secrecy are peeled back, a new perspective emerges on the intersection of medicine, politics, and historical legacy. The intricate web of cover-ups, misdirection, and carefully crafted public images reveals the profound impact that private struggles can have on public events. The story unfolds with a sense of urgency, inviting reflection on the ethical boundaries of leadership and the consequences of withholding truth from a nation in crisis. In this exploration, the boundaries between personal vulnerability and public responsibility blur, offering a deeper understanding of how history can be shaped as much by what is hidden as by what is revealed.

**What You’ll Get Out of This Book**

You will discover the hidden medical history of FDR, gain insight into how secrecy and health influenced pivotal decisions, and develop a sharper awareness of the ethical complexities surrounding leadership and transparency in times of national crisis. Expect to see history through a more critical and revealing lens.
