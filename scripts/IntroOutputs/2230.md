**Introduction:**

Patterns of daily life, from commuting to healthcare, often reveal a hidden architecture shaped by data that overlooks half the population. In a world where decisions are made based on information that defaults to the male experience, the consequences ripple through every aspect of society. Caroline Criado Pérez’s Invisible Women: Data Bias in a World Designed for Men brings to light the profound impact of this gender data gap, exposing how the absence of women’s perspectives in research, policy, and design leads to environments that systematically disadvantage women. The narrative uncovers the ways in which this bias is not just a matter of oversight, but a deeply embedded feature of modern systems, affecting safety, health, and economic opportunity.

As the layers of this unseen bias are peeled back, the interconnectedness of seemingly neutral choices becomes clear. From public transportation schedules to medical protocols, the evidence mounts that the world is calibrated to a male norm, often at the expense of women’s well-being and participation. The journey through these revelations is both eye-opening and urgent, challenging assumptions about objectivity and fairness in the structures that govern everyday life. With each example, the call for more inclusive data and thoughtful design grows stronger, inviting a reimagining of what true equality could look like. The exploration of these themes sets the stage for a deeper understanding of the pervasive influence of gendered data bias.

**What You’ll Get Out of This Book**

You will discover how gender bias in data shapes the world around you, often in invisible ways. You can expect to gain a sharper awareness of systemic inequalities, practical examples of their real-world impact, and a new perspective on the importance of inclusive research and design for building a fairer society.
