**Introduction:**

Visions of economic democracy and collective ownership have long inspired those seeking alternatives to traditional capitalist structures. In the context of the United States, the idea of worker cooperatives emerges as both a historical reality and a radical possibility, challenging the dominance of hierarchical workplaces and profit-driven priorities. Worker Cooperatives and Revolution: History and Possibilities in the United States by Chris Wright delves into the roots and evolution of worker-owned enterprises, tracing their presence from early experiments to contemporary movements. The narrative explores how these cooperatives have not only provided economic security and empowerment for their members but have also served as laboratories for broader social transformation.

As the story unfolds, the interplay between grassroots organizing, political resistance, and the quest for economic justice comes into sharp focus. The journey through the successes and setbacks of worker cooperatives reveals the persistent tension between revolutionary aspirations and the realities of operating within a capitalist society. By examining both the historical record and the untapped potential of worker cooperatives, the discussion opens up new avenues for imagining a more equitable and participatory future. The exploration of these themes invites a deeper consideration of what it means to build power from below and to reimagine the very foundations of work and ownership in America.

**What You’ll Get Out of This Book**

You will gain a nuanced understanding of the history, challenges, and transformative potential of worker cooperatives in the United States. You can expect to discover how collective ownership models have shaped—and could continue to shape—movements for economic justice and social change.
