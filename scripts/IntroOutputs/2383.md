**Introduction:**

The unsettling terrain of existence and nonexistence comes into sharp focus through the lens of antinatalism, a philosophy that questions the value of bringing new life into the world. Better Never to Have Been: The Harm of Coming into Existence by David Benatar delves into the profound ethical dilemma of whether birth itself is a harm, challenging the deeply held assumption that life is inherently good. The argument unfolds around the asymmetry between pain and pleasure, suggesting that the absence of suffering is always good, even if no one benefits from it, while the absence of pleasure is not bad unless someone is deprived of it. This perspective invites a radical reconsideration of procreation, personal identity, and the responsibilities of those who choose to create life.

Exploring the implications of this view, the discussion traverses philosophical, psychological, and existential dimensions, questioning the morality of bringing sentient beings into a world where suffering is inevitable. The narrative weaves together logical reasoning, ethical analysis, and real-world consequences, prompting reflection on the nature of harm, consent, and the meaning of existence itself. As the argument unfolds, it becomes clear that the question of whether it is better never to have been is not merely abstract, but strikes at the heart of human experience and the choices that shape future generations.

**What You’ll Get Out of This Book**

You will gain a thought-provoking perspective on the ethics of procreation, challenging conventional beliefs about the value of life. Expect to confront deep philosophical questions about suffering, consent, and existence, and to develop a nuanced understanding of antinatalist arguments and their implications for personal and societal choices.
