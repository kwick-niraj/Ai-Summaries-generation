**Introduction:**

Racist ideas have shaped the fabric of American society, influencing laws, institutions, and everyday interactions for centuries. The journey through the nation’s past reveals how these ideas were not simply inherited but actively constructed, debated, and reinforced by powerful interests. Stamped from the Beginning: The Definitive History of Racist Ideas in America by Ibram X. Kendi traces the origins and evolution of these beliefs, exposing the deliberate strategies that have perpetuated racial inequality. The narrative uncovers the ways in which racism has been justified, rationalized, and even disguised as progress, challenging the notion that the arc of history naturally bends toward justice.

Through the lives and works of key historical figures, the story unfolds across generations, showing how racist ideas have adapted to changing times while maintaining their grip on the nation’s consciousness. The interplay between policy, culture, and personal conviction emerges, revealing the complexity of combating deeply rooted prejudice. As the narrative moves from colonial America to the present, it becomes clear that understanding the history of racist ideas is essential for confronting their enduring legacy. The exploration invites a deeper reckoning with the past, setting the stage for meaningful change and a more equitable future.

**What You’ll Get Out of This Book**

You will gain a comprehensive understanding of how racist ideas have been created, spread, and sustained throughout American history. You can expect to develop a sharper awareness of the forces that shape racial attitudes and discover new perspectives for challenging prejudice and fostering social justice.
