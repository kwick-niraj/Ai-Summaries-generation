**Introduction:**

In the shadowy streets of Victorian London, the lives of five women intersected with one of history’s most infamous murderers, yet their own stories have long been eclipsed by the legend of Jack the Ripper. The Five: The Untold Lives of the Women Killed by Jack the Ripper by Hallie Rubenhold brings to light the overlooked humanity, struggles, and resilience of Polly, Annie, Elizabeth, Catherine, and Mary Jane. Their experiences reflect the harsh realities faced by countless women in a society marked by poverty, prejudice, and rigid social hierarchies. Each woman’s journey reveals the complexities of survival in a world that often denied them agency and dignity, challenging the simplistic labels that history has imposed upon them.

Through the lens of these five lives, a vivid portrait emerges of Victorian England’s underbelly, where economic hardship, family breakdown, and societal neglect shaped destinies as much as any notorious crime. The narrative moves beyond the sensationalism of the murders to explore the dreams, disappointments, and daily battles of women whose voices have been lost to time. In reclaiming their stories, a deeper understanding unfolds—not only of the victims themselves, but of the world that failed them. This perspective invites a reconsideration of history’s priorities and a recognition of the enduring impact of compassion and justice.

**What You’ll Get Out of This Book**

You will discover the untold stories and humanity of the women known as Jack the Ripper’s victims, gaining a deeper understanding of their lives, struggles, and the social forces that shaped their destinies. You can expect to gain empathy, historical insight, and a fresh perspective on Victorian society.
