**Introduction:**

The boundaries between human and nonhuman animals have long shaped moral thinking, yet the question of how to treat other sentient beings remains urgent and unresolved. Animal Liberation by Peter Singer challenges the deeply ingrained assumptions that place human interests above those of animals, urging a reconsideration of the ethical frameworks that justify their use for food, experimentation, and entertainment. The notion of speciesism—discrimination based on species membership—emerges as a central concern, inviting reflection on the parallels between historical struggles for equality and the ongoing plight of animals. Compassion, rationality, and justice intertwine, prompting a reexamination of daily choices and the systems that support them.

Within this landscape, the realities of factory farming, animal testing, and the commodification of life come into sharp focus. The suffering experienced by animals is not an abstract concept but a tangible consequence of collective habits and institutional practices. Arguments for animal rights and welfare are weighed against cultural traditions and economic interests, revealing the complexity of change. As the implications of empathy and ethical consistency unfold, the call to action becomes clear: to expand the circle of moral concern and confront the uncomfortable truths that lie beneath the surface of ordinary life.

**What You’ll Get Out of This Book**

You will discover how ethical reasoning can be applied to the treatment of animals, gain insight into the realities of animal suffering, and learn to recognize the impact of everyday choices. Expect to challenge assumptions, broaden your sense of justice, and find practical guidance for living with greater compassion.
