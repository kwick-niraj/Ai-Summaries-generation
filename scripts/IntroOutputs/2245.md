**Introduction:**

Curiosity about why things happen, not just how, has always driven human progress. The Book of Why: The New Science of Cause and Effect by Judea Pearl delves into the heart of this quest, exploring the profound difference between correlation and causation. The journey moves beyond mere patterns in data, seeking to understand the underlying mechanisms that govern the world. At its core, the exploration centers on the human ability to ask "why"—to imagine alternate realities, to intervene, and to predict the consequences of actions. This capacity for causal reasoning shapes everything from scientific discovery to everyday decision-making, revealing the hidden logic that connects events and outcomes.

As the landscape of science and technology evolves, so too does the understanding of causality. The narrative traces the evolution of thought from ancient philosophical debates to the cutting-edge algorithms that now power artificial intelligence. Through vivid examples and groundbreaking insights, the world of cause and effect unfolds, showing how new tools and frameworks are transforming fields as diverse as medicine, economics, and social science. The journey through these ideas illuminates not only how questions are answered, but how new questions are formed—inviting a deeper appreciation for the power and promise of causal thinking.

**What You’ll Get Out of This Book**

You will learn how to distinguish between correlation and causation, uncover the principles behind causal reasoning, and discover practical frameworks for making better decisions in science, technology, and everyday life. You can expect to gain a new perspective on how questions are asked and answered in a world driven by cause and effect.
