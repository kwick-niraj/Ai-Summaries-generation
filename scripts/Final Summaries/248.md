# What I Wish I Said: Confessions of a Columnist — Summary (Part 1)

---

## Chapter 1: The Unspoken Truths Behind Everyday Advice

There’s a certain comfort in reading advice columns or listening to someone share their thoughts on life’s dilemmas. The words often sound polished, the answers neat, as if every problem can be wrapped up with a bow. But beneath those tidy responses, there’s a world of hesitation, second-guessing, and the things left unsaid. Most people don’t realize how much is held back—how much of the real story never makes it to the page or the microphone.

Imagine someone sitting at a kitchen table, pen in hand, staring at a blank page. The question in front of them is simple enough: “How do I handle disappointment?” The answer that eventually gets written is practical, maybe even wise. But what’s left out is the memory of a recent letdown, the sting of a friend’s broken promise, the quiet tears shed in the car on the way home. The advice is true, but it’s only the surface. Underneath, there’s a confession: sometimes, the person giving the advice is still figuring it out, too.

This is the tension that runs through so many public conversations. The person on the platform, the one with the microphone or the byline, is expected to have it all together. But behind the scenes, there’s a private world of doubts, fears, and unfinished thoughts. The gap between what’s said and what’s really felt can be wide. It’s easy to forget that the person offering guidance is just as human as anyone else.

In the quiet moments after the column is published or the podcast episode airs, there’s often a lingering sense of “I wish I’d said more.” Not because the advice was wrong, but because it was incomplete. There’s a longing to share the messy parts—the moments of weakness, the prayers whispered in the dark, the times when faith feels thin and answers are hard to find.

One evening, a woman sits with her phone, scrolling through messages from people who’ve read her latest piece. Some thank her for her honesty, others ask for more details, a few share their own struggles. She feels both grateful and exposed. It’s a reminder that real connection happens not when everything is polished, but when someone dares to be vulnerable.

Most people crave this kind of honesty. They want to know that they’re not alone in their confusion or pain. They want to hear that even those who seem wise and put-together have their own battles. There’s a quiet power in admitting, “I don’t have all the answers.” It opens the door for real conversation, for grace, for growth.

Faith, in these moments, becomes less about having the perfect response and more about showing up with authenticity. It’s about trusting that God’s grace is big enough to cover the gaps, to fill in the places where words fall short. The real work happens not in the polished advice, but in the willingness to confess what’s really going on inside.

Over time, it becomes clear that the most meaningful connections are built on honesty. The advice that sticks isn’t always the most eloquent—it’s the most real. When someone dares to share their struggles, their doubts, their hopes, it creates space for others to do the same. The unspoken truths, once brought into the light, have a way of healing not just the person who shares them, but everyone who listens.

## Chapter 2: The Cost and Freedom of Vulnerability

There’s a certain risk in letting others see the parts of life that aren’t picture-perfect. Most people are taught, from an early age, to keep their struggles hidden, to present a strong front. But the truth is, everyone carries wounds—some fresh, some old and scarred over. The decision to be vulnerable, to let those wounds show, is never easy.

Picture a young professional, sitting in a crowded office, feeling the weight of expectations. On the outside, everything looks fine—deadlines are met, smiles are given, small talk flows easily. But inside, there’s a storm of anxiety and self-doubt. The temptation is to keep it all bottled up, to pretend that nothing is wrong. After all, who wants to be seen as weak or needy?

But then, in a quiet moment, a trusted colleague asks, “Are you okay?” The question lingers, heavy with possibility. There’s a choice to be made: keep up the façade, or let the truth slip out. When the words finally come—halting, uncertain, but real—something shifts. The mask drops, and for the first time in a long while, there’s relief. The burden is lighter, simply because it’s been shared.

This is the paradox of vulnerability. It feels risky, even dangerous, but it’s also the path to freedom. Most people don’t realize how much energy it takes to keep up appearances. The effort to hide pain or confusion can be exhausting. But when someone finally admits, “I’m struggling,” the pressure eases. There’s room to breathe, to heal, to grow.

In faith communities, this tension is especially strong. There’s often an unspoken expectation to be joyful, to have it all together, to trust God without wavering. But real life is messier than that. There are days when prayers go unanswered, when doubts creep in, when hope feels distant. Pretending otherwise only deepens the sense of isolation.

One Sunday, a woman sits in the back row of a church, fighting back tears. The sermon is about perseverance, about trusting God in hard times. She wants to believe it, but her heart is heavy with grief. After the service, someone asks how she’s doing. For a moment, she considers giving the usual answer—“I’m fine.” But instead, she lets the truth spill out. The conversation that follows is raw, but it’s also healing. In that moment, faith becomes real—not because everything is fixed, but because the struggle is shared.

Over time, it becomes clear that vulnerability is not a weakness, but a strength. It takes courage to admit when things aren’t okay, to ask for help, to let others see the real story. The freedom that comes from honesty is worth the risk. It creates space for genuine connection, for empathy, for grace.

Most people long for this kind of authenticity. They want to know that they’re not alone in their struggles, that others have walked the same hard roads. When someone dares to be vulnerable, it gives permission for others to do the same. The cost is real—there’s always a chance of being misunderstood or rejected. But the freedom that comes from being known, truly known, is worth it.

## Chapter 3: Faith in the Ordinary and the Mundane

It’s easy to think of faith as something reserved for the big moments—the crises, the miracles, the mountaintop experiences. But most of life happens in the ordinary, in the routines and rhythms that fill each day. The challenge is to find meaning, and even holiness, in the mundane.

Consider a parent, up before dawn, packing lunches and searching for missing shoes. The morning rush is chaotic, filled with spilled milk and forgotten homework. There’s little time for quiet reflection or grand gestures of faith. Yet, in these small acts of care, something sacred is happening. The decision to show up, day after day, to love and serve without fanfare, is a kind of worship.

Most people overlook the spiritual significance of these moments. They wait for something dramatic, some clear sign of God’s presence. But faith is often found in the unnoticed places—in the patience shown to a difficult child, in the forgiveness offered after an argument, in the simple act of making dinner for a tired spouse. These are the places where grace takes root.

One afternoon, a woman stands at the sink, washing dishes for what feels like the hundredth time that week. Her mind drifts to the dreams she once had, the plans that never quite materialized. It’s tempting to believe that her life is small, insignificant. But then, a quiet thought surfaces: every act of service, no matter how ordinary, matters. The mundane becomes meaningful when it’s offered with love.

Faith in the ordinary is not about ignoring pain or pretending that everything is fine. It’s about trusting that God is present, even in the mess. It’s about believing that small acts of kindness, done in secret, have eternal value. Most people underestimate the impact of these moments. They look for purpose in the extraordinary, but often miss the beauty of the everyday.

Over time, it becomes clear that the line between sacred and secular is thinner than most realize. The kitchen table can be an altar, the commute a time of prayer, the bedtime story a chance to speak truth and hope. Faith is not just for Sundays or special occasions—it’s woven into the fabric of daily life.

The challenge is to pay attention, to look for God in the ordinary. It’s easy to get lost in routine, to let the days blur together. But when someone chooses to see the sacred in the mundane, everything changes. The ordinary becomes extraordinary, not because the circumstances are different, but because the perspective is.

## Chapter 4: The Tension Between Public Persona and Private Reality

In a world where so much of life is lived online, the gap between public image and private reality can feel overwhelming. Social media, with its carefully curated photos and status updates, creates the illusion that everyone else has it all together. But behind the screens, the truth is often much messier.

Picture someone scrolling through their feed late at night, comparing their real life to the highlight reels of others. The temptation is to present a version of themselves that’s polished and impressive, to hide the struggles and insecurities. But the cost of this performance is high. The more someone tries to maintain a perfect image, the more isolated they feel.

This tension is especially strong for those in the public eye. The pressure to be a role model, to have all the answers, can be suffocating. There’s a constant fear of being exposed, of letting people down. The private doubts and failures are kept hidden, locked away where no one can see.

One afternoon, a woman sits in her car after a speaking engagement, exhausted and anxious. The applause has faded, the compliments still ringing in her ears. But inside, she feels empty, unsure if she really believes the words she just spoke. The disconnect between her public persona and private reality is stark. It’s a lonely place to be.

Most people don’t realize how common this experience is. The pressure to perform, to meet expectations, is universal. But the truth is, everyone has a private world that doesn’t always match the image they present. The challenge is to bridge the gap, to let the public and private selves come closer together.

Over time, it becomes clear that authenticity is the antidote to this tension. The decision to be honest, to let others see the real story, is freeing. It doesn’t mean sharing every detail with everyone, but it does mean refusing to pretend. The most powerful moments often come when someone admits, “I don’t have it all together.” It’s in these moments that real connection happens.

Faith plays a crucial role in this process. It offers the security to be honest, to trust that worth is not based on performance or approval. God’s grace covers the gaps, the failures, the places where the image falls short. The journey toward authenticity is ongoing, but each step brings more freedom, more peace.

Most people long for this kind of honesty, both in themselves and in others. The world doesn’t need more perfect images—it needs real stories, real struggles, real hope. When someone dares to close the gap between public and private, it creates space for others to do the same. The result is a community built not on appearances, but on truth.

## Chapter 5: Growth Through Confession and Authenticity

There’s a quiet power in owning what hasn’t been said. Most people carry secrets—regrets, fears, hopes that never find their way into conversation. The act of confession, of bringing these hidden things into the light, is both terrifying and liberating.

Imagine someone sitting across from a friend, heart pounding, words caught in their throat. There’s something important to say, something that’s been weighing on them for months. The fear of rejection is real, but so is the longing to be known. When the words finally come, there’s a sense of release. The secret loses its power, and the relationship deepens.

Confession is not just about admitting mistakes—it’s about telling the truth, even when it’s hard. It’s about acknowledging the places where things have gone wrong, where pain has been caused, where healing is needed. Most people avoid these conversations, afraid of what might happen. But the cost of silence is high. Secrets fester, relationships suffer, growth stalls.

In faith communities, confession is often seen as a spiritual discipline, a way to make things right with God and others. But it’s also a deeply human act. It’s a way of saying, “I want to be real, even if it’s messy.” The process is rarely easy. There are tears, apologies, sometimes even anger. But on the other side, there’s freedom.

One evening, a woman sits with her journal, writing out the things she’s never said. The list is long—old hurts, unspoken fears, dreams deferred. As she writes, something shifts. The act of naming these things, of bringing them into the open, is healing. It’s a reminder that growth comes not from hiding, but from honesty.

Over time, it becomes clear that authenticity is the key to real growth. The willingness to confess, to own the truth, creates space for change. It’s not about being perfect, but about being real. Most people are drawn to this kind of honesty. They want to know that it’s possible to be both broken and beloved, to struggle and still be accepted.

Faith offers a foundation for this kind of growth. God’s grace is big enough to handle the truth, to cover the failures and the fears. The journey is ongoing—there will always be more to confess, more to learn, more to heal. But each step toward authenticity brings more freedom, more connection, more hope.

The power of confession is not just in the words spoken, but in the courage it takes to speak them. When someone dares to be honest, it creates space for others to do the same. The result is a community marked by grace, by truth, by real growth.

## Chapter 6: The Stories We Carry and the Weight of Silence

Sometimes, the stories that shape us are the ones we never tell. They sit quietly in the background, coloring our choices and our fears, shaping the way we see ourselves and others. Most people have a handful of these stories—moments of shame, of loss, of longing—that never quite find their way into the open. The silence can feel protective, but it’s also heavy.

Picture someone at a family gathering, laughter and conversation swirling around them. There’s a story they want to share, a memory that aches to be spoken, but the words catch in their throat. Maybe it’s the fear of being misunderstood, or the worry that their pain will be too much for others to bear. So they smile, nod, and tuck the story away for another day.

Over time, the weight of these untold stories grows. It becomes harder to connect, harder to feel seen. The silence builds walls, even with those closest to us. Most people don’t realize how much of their loneliness comes from the things they never say.

But every so often, the right moment comes—a late-night conversation, a long walk with a friend, a letter written in the quiet hours. The story slips out, tentative and trembling. There’s a pause, a breath held, and then a gentle response: “Me too.” In that moment, the weight lifts. The story, once hidden, becomes a bridge.

It becomes clear that sharing our stories, even the hard ones, is an act of courage. It’s a way of saying, “This is who I am. This is what I’ve lived.” The risk is real—there’s always the chance of being hurt. But the reward is connection, understanding, and sometimes even healing.

Faith, in these moments, is about trusting that our stories matter. That God sees the parts we hide, and loves us anyway. The journey toward wholeness begins when we dare to let our stories be known, trusting that we are not alone.

## Chapter 7: Navigating Doubt and the Search for Belonging

Doubt is a quiet companion for many, especially in matters of faith. It slips in during the hard seasons, whispering questions that don’t have easy answers. Most people are taught to push doubt aside, to pretend it doesn’t exist. But the truth is, doubt is part of being human.

Imagine someone sitting in a crowded sanctuary, surrounded by voices singing songs of certainty. Their own voice falters, the words catching in their throat. They wonder if they belong, if their questions disqualify them from the community they long to be part of. The fear of being found out—of being labeled as “less than”—is real.

But doubt, when faced honestly, can be a doorway to deeper faith. It’s in the wrestling, the searching, that faith becomes real. Most people don’t realize that even the strongest believers have moments of uncertainty. The difference is in how they respond—whether they hide their questions, or bring them into the light.

One evening, a small group gathers in a living room, Bibles open, coffee cups in hand. The conversation turns to a difficult passage, and someone admits, “I don’t understand this. Sometimes I’m not even sure what I believe.” There’s a moment of silence, and then others begin to share their own doubts. The room fills with relief, with laughter, with a sense of belonging.

It becomes clear that faith is not about having all the answers. It’s about showing up, even with questions. It’s about trusting that God is big enough to handle our doubts, and that community is strong enough to hold our uncertainty.

Over time, the search for belonging becomes less about fitting in, and more about being known. The most welcoming communities are not the ones with the fewest questions, but the ones with the most honesty. Doubt, when shared, becomes a thread that weaves people together.

Faith grows in the soil of honesty. The journey is ongoing—there will always be more questions, more searching. But each step toward vulnerability brings more connection, more hope.

## Chapter 8: The Role of Faith in a Shifting Culture

The world is changing fast. Values shift, traditions are questioned, and the old certainties don’t always hold. For many, faith feels like an anchor in the storm—a way to find meaning and direction when everything else is in flux. But holding on to faith in a changing culture is not always easy.

Picture someone scrolling through headlines, feeling the weight of division and uncertainty. The world feels fractured, and it’s tempting to retreat, to build walls and cling to what’s familiar. But faith, at its best, is not about withdrawal. It’s about engagement—about bringing hope and compassion into the places that need it most.

Most people don’t realize how much their faith is shaped by the culture around them. The challenge is to hold on to what matters, while staying open to new perspectives. It’s a delicate balance—honoring tradition, while making space for growth and change.

One afternoon, a group of friends gathers around a table, debating the issues of the day. The conversation is lively, sometimes heated. But underneath the disagreement is a shared commitment to listen, to learn, to love. Faith, in this context, is not a weapon—it’s a bridge.

It becomes clear that the role of faith in culture is not to dominate, but to serve. It’s about showing up with humility, with curiosity, with a willingness to be changed. The most powerful witness is not in having all the answers, but in loving well.

Over time, the journey becomes less about defending beliefs, and more about living them out. The world doesn’t need more arguments—it needs more kindness, more justice, more grace. Faith, when lived authentically, becomes a force for good.

The challenge is to stay rooted, even as the world shifts. To trust that God is at work, even in the chaos. The journey is ongoing, but each step toward engagement brings more hope, more connection.

## Chapter 9: The Ongoing Work of Forgiveness

Forgiveness is one of the hardest things to practice, even for those who talk about it often. Most people carry wounds—some fresh, some old and festering. The decision to forgive, to let go of resentment, is never simple. It’s a process, not a one-time event.

Imagine someone replaying an old hurt, the words still sharp after all these years. The temptation is to hold on, to nurse the wound, to wait for an apology that may never come. But the cost of unforgiveness is high. It seeps into relationships, into health, into the way we see ourselves.

One day, after years of silence, a letter arrives. The words are simple, but they open a door. There’s a choice to be made: hold on to the pain, or take a step toward healing. The process is slow—sometimes it feels like two steps forward, one step back. But with each act of forgiveness, the burden grows lighter.

Most people don’t realize that forgiveness is as much for the giver as for the receiver. It’s a way of setting ourselves free, of making space for new growth. The journey is rarely easy. There are setbacks, moments of anger, times when the old pain resurfaces. But over time, the grip of resentment loosens.

Faith offers a foundation for forgiveness. It’s the belief that grace is possible, even when it feels undeserved. The process is ongoing—there will always be more to forgive, more to let go. But each act of forgiveness brings more freedom, more peace.

It becomes clear that forgiveness is not about forgetting, or pretending that the hurt didn’t happen. It’s about choosing to move forward, to release the hold of the past. The journey is long, but the destination is worth it.

## Chapter 10: Wholeness, Connection, and the Courage to Begin Again

Life is full of endings and beginnings. Relationships change, dreams shift, seasons come and go. Most people long for a sense of wholeness—a feeling that their life, with all its mess and beauty, is enough. But wholeness is not about perfection. It’s about integration, about bringing all the parts of ourselves—past and present, joy and pain—into the light.

Picture someone standing at a crossroads, unsure of which way to go. The past is full of regrets, the future uncertain. The temptation is to stay stuck, to let fear or shame hold them back. But there’s another way—a way of courage, of choosing to begin again.

One morning, after a long night of wrestling with old fears, a decision is made. It’s not dramatic—just a quiet resolve to take the next step, to reach out, to try again. The process is slow, sometimes halting. But with each small act of courage, the sense of wholeness grows.

Most people don’t realize that connection is built in these small moments—in the willingness to be seen, to be known, to risk vulnerability. The journey toward wholeness is not a solo endeavor. It’s a path walked with others, with all their flaws and gifts.

Faith, in these moments, is about trusting that new beginnings are possible. That grace is real, and that the story is not over. The journey is ongoing—there will always be more to learn, more to heal, more to hope for.

Over time, it becomes clear that the courage to begin again is the heart of growth. The willingness to try, to fail, to get up and try again, is what makes life rich and meaningful. Wholeness is not a destination, but a way of living—with honesty, with hope, with love.

## Conclusion: What I Wish I Said—And What You Can Say Now

If there’s one thread running through all these confessions, it’s this: the things left unsaid are often the most important. The journey toward authenticity, connection, and faith is not about having perfect answers or polished stories. It’s about daring to speak, to listen, to show up as you are.

Here are a few gentle reminders to carry with you:

- Vulnerability is a risk, but it’s also the path to real connection.
- Your ordinary moments are sacred, even when they feel small or unseen.
- Doubt and faith can coexist; questions are not a sign of failure.
- Forgiveness is a process—give yourself time and grace to heal.
- Wholeness comes from embracing your story, not hiding from it.

You don’t have to wait for the perfect moment to speak your truth. The courage to share, to confess, to reach out, is available right now. The world is hungry for honesty—for stories that are messy, unfinished, and real. When you dare to say what’s on your heart, you create space for others to do the same. You become a bridge, a light, a reminder that none of us are alone.

So take the next step, however small. Write the letter, make the call, start the conversation. Trust that your story matters, that your voice is needed. The journey is ongoing, and there will be setbacks. But each act of honesty, each moment of courage, brings more freedom, more connection, more hope.

You are not alone in your questions, your struggles, your longing for meaning. There is grace enough for every unfinished sentence, every unspoken word. The invitation is simple: show up, as you are, and let your story be heard. The rest will unfold, one honest word at a time.
