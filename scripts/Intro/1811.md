## Introduction

Economic decisions ripple through society, shaping lives in ways both immediate and far-reaching. The world of Economics in One Lesson by Henry Hazlitt invites a closer look at the unseen consequences of policies and the importance of thinking beyond short-term gains. At its heart lies a simple yet profound principle: every economic action has effects not just on one group, but on all groups, and not just in the present, but in the future as well. This perspective challenges the allure of quick fixes and urges a deeper understanding of how incentives, costs, and benefits interact across the entire economic landscape.

Through clear examples and sharp reasoning, the journey unfolds across familiar topics—taxes, government spending, price controls, and more—revealing the hidden costs and unintended outcomes that often escape notice. Hazlitt’s approach encourages a habit of looking past surface-level benefits to consider the broader, long-term impact of economic choices. By following this thread, the interconnectedness of markets, policies, and human behavior comes into focus, offering a lens through which to evaluate not just what is seen, but what remains unseen. With this foundation, the path opens toward a more thoughtful and responsible engagement with the economic forces that shape everyday life.
