## Introduction

Power and its relationship to gender have shaped societies for millennia, often dictating who is heard and who is silenced. The persistent question of why women’s voices are so frequently marginalized, and how authority is constructed and maintained, lies at the heart of Women & Power: A Manifesto by Mary Beard. Drawing on historical precedent and cultural narratives, the conversation turns to the ways in which women have been excluded from the corridors of influence, and how language, myth, and tradition have reinforced these boundaries. The echoes of ancient stories still reverberate in modern debates, revealing the deep roots of contemporary challenges faced by women seeking agency and recognition.

As the lens shifts from the past to the present, the patterns of resistance and adaptation become clear. The struggle for women’s power is not just about individual achievement, but about redefining the very structures that determine who gets to speak and who is expected to listen. Through incisive analysis and vivid examples, the journey unfolds from the silencing of women in classical antiquity to the ongoing battles in public and private spheres today. The call for a new vision of power—one that is more inclusive, flexible, and open to transformation—emerges as both urgent and possible, inviting a reconsideration of what true authority can look like.
