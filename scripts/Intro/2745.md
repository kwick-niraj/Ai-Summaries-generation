## Introduction

The shadow of World War Two stretches far beyond the battlefield, lingering in the unspoken truths and hidden tragedies that shaped the destinies of nations. In the world of Who Has Buried the Dead?: From Stalin to Putin … The last great secret of World War Two by K.G.E. Konkel, the enduring mysteries of the war’s aftermath come to life, revealing how the manipulation of memory and the suppression of evidence have influenced the course of Russian history. The narrative delves into the complex interplay between power, secrecy, and the relentless pursuit of truth, exploring how the legacy of Stalin’s regime continues to echo through the corridors of the Kremlin, even as new leaders rise and fall.

As the story unfolds, the search for answers becomes a journey through decades of political intrigue, state-sponsored deception, and the personal courage of those who dare to challenge official narratives. The fate of the dead—both literally and metaphorically—serves as a powerful symbol for the unresolved questions that haunt Russia’s past and present. Through the lens of this enduring enigma, the consequences of historical denial and the cost of uncovering buried secrets are laid bare, inviting reflection on the ways in which history is written, rewritten, and sometimes willfully forgotten. The stage is set for a deeper exploration of the forces that have shaped modern Russia and the enduring impact of World War Two’s most closely guarded secrets.
