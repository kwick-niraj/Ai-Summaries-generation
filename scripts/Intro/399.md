## Introduction

A world shaped by poverty, hunger, and the ever-present shadow of racial prejudice forms the backdrop for Richard Wright’s Black Boy. The journey unfolds through the eyes of a young Black child growing up in the Jim Crow South, where every day is a negotiation between survival and dignity. The struggle to find one’s voice amid silence and oppression becomes a central thread, as the search for meaning and selfhood collides with the harsh realities of family, community, and a society built on exclusion. The longing for knowledge and the power of words emerge as both a refuge and a weapon, offering glimpses of hope in a world that often seems determined to crush it.

As the story moves from the rural South to the urban North, the challenges shift but never disappear. Encounters with violence, hunger, and alienation are met with a fierce determination to understand and transcend the forces that seek to define and limit. The journey is marked by moments of rebellion, discovery, and the relentless pursuit of personal truth. Through the lens of one life, the broader questions of race, identity, and the cost of freedom are brought into sharp focus, inviting reflection on the enduring struggle for dignity and belonging.
