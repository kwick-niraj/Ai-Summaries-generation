## Introduction

The struggle to find meaning in a world that often appears indifferent or even absurd lies at the heart of human experience. Questions about existence, purpose, and the search for value in a universe that offers no clear answers can provoke both despair and liberation. In The Myth of Sisyphus and Other Essays by Albert Camus, the confrontation with the absurd becomes a central theme, exploring how individuals respond when faced with the realization that life’s search for meaning may ultimately be met with silence. The ancient story of Sisyphus, condemned to endlessly roll a boulder uphill only to watch it tumble down again, becomes a powerful metaphor for the human condition—one marked by persistent effort in the face of futility.

Through a blend of philosophical reflection and literary insight, Camus delves into the ways people grapple with the absurd, examining the tension between the desire for clarity and the world’s inherent ambiguity. The essays traverse a landscape of existential thought, considering rebellion, freedom, and the possibility of happiness even when confronted with life’s apparent meaninglessness. The journey through these ideas invites a deeper understanding of how to live authentically, embracing the absurd not as a source of despair, but as a foundation for personal strength and creative defiance.
