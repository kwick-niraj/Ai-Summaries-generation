## Introduction

Assumptions about reality often feel unshakable, yet history reveals how frequently the obvious truths of one era become the laughable misconceptions of the next. In But What If We're Wrong? Thinking About the Present As If It Were the Past, Chuck Klosterman invites a radical shift in perspective, challenging the certainty with which people view their own time. The world is built on a foundation of beliefs—about science, culture, art, and even the nature of existence itself—that seem irrefutable until, inevitably, they are not. By questioning the permanence of what is taken for granted, a space opens up for curiosity, humility, and the possibility that the future will see the present as misguided or naive.

Exploring everything from the fate of rock music to the nature of gravity, Klosterman weaves together philosophy, pop culture, and speculative thought experiments. The familiar becomes strange when viewed through the lens of future hindsight, and the boundaries between certainty and doubt blur. What if the most basic convictions—about literature, sports, or even the shape of the universe—are destined to be overturned? This journey through uncertainty encourages a playful skepticism, inviting a deeper engagement with the mysteries of the present and a readiness to embrace the unknown.
