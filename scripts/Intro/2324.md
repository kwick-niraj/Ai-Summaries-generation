## Introduction

The Denial of Death by Ernest Becker delves into the profound human struggle with mortality, exploring how the awareness of death shapes behavior, culture, and the psyche. At the heart of existence lies a paradox: the drive to achieve meaning and significance, shadowed by the inescapable reality of impermanence. This tension gives rise to powerful psychological defenses, as individuals and societies construct elaborate systems—religion, heroism, and cultural myths—to shield themselves from the terror of nonexistence. The quest for self-preservation and symbolic immortality becomes a central force, influencing everything from personal ambition to collective identity.

As the layers of denial and repression are examined, the intricate dance between fear and creativity emerges. The search for purpose, the need for validation, and the impulse to transcend the limitations of the physical body all reflect a deep-seated desire to overcome the finality of death. Becker’s exploration reveals how confronting mortality can lead to both existential anxiety and profound liberation, inviting a reimagining of what it means to live authentically in the face of the inevitable. The journey through these ideas opens a path toward understanding the roots of human motivation and the possibility of embracing life with greater courage and clarity.
