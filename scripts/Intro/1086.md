## Introduction

The enduring questions of faith, reason, and morality find themselves at the heart of a relentless inquiry, where the influence of organized religion is scrutinized through the lens of history, culture, and personal experience. In God Is Not Great: How Religion Poisons Everything, Christopher Hitchens confronts the complex legacy of religious belief, challenging the notion that faith is inherently virtuous or necessary for ethical living. The narrative unfolds across centuries and continents, examining the ways in which religious dogma has shaped societies, inspired both compassion and cruelty, and intersected with politics, science, and human rights.

Hitchens’s exploration moves beyond mere critique, delving into the psychological and societal mechanisms that allow religious ideas to persist and dominate. The interplay between skepticism and tradition, the tension between inquiry and doctrine, and the consequences of unquestioned belief are all brought into sharp focus. Through vivid examples and incisive argument, the journey reveals how the search for meaning and truth can flourish outside the boundaries of religious orthodoxy, inviting a deeper reflection on the sources of morality, the value of doubt, and the possibilities of a life guided by reason. The stage is set for a sweeping examination of faith’s impact on the world and the individual.
