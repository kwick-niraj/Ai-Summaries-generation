## Introduction

Everyday objects, from doors to digital interfaces, shape the rhythm of daily life, yet their design often goes unnoticed until frustration arises. The world is filled with products that either empower or hinder, depending on how intuitively they fit into human habits and expectations. The Design of Everyday Things by Donald A. Norman delves into the subtle dance between human psychology and the artifacts that populate modern existence, revealing how thoughtful design can transform confusion into clarity and irritation into delight. The interplay of usability, aesthetics, and function becomes a lens through which the invisible rules of good design are brought to light.

Exploring the reasons behind both seamless and maddening user experiences, the journey moves through the principles that make objects understandable and usable. Concepts such as affordances, feedback, and mapping emerge as guiding stars, illuminating why some products feel natural while others baffle. The narrative uncovers the cognitive processes that govern interaction with the material world, highlighting the importance of empathy and observation in crafting solutions that truly serve. As the exploration unfolds, the promise of a more harmonious relationship between people and their tools comes into focus, inviting a reimagining of the ordinary.
