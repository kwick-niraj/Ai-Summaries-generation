## Introduction

Visions of a better world have long inspired both dreamers and pragmatists, fueling debates about what is truly possible for society. Utopia for Realists: How We Can Build the Ideal World by Rutger Bregman delves into the radical notion that many ideas once dismissed as utopian—universal basic income, a fifteen-hour workweek, and open borders—are not only achievable but may be essential for progress. The narrative challenges the boundaries of imagination and practicality, inviting a reexamination of what is considered realistic in the pursuit of collective well-being.

Exploring the intersection of history, economics, and social innovation, the journey unfolds through bold proposals and real-world experiments that have already begun to reshape communities. The persistent question emerges: why settle for the status quo when evidence and creativity can point toward a more just and prosperous future? As the contours of possibility expand, the conversation shifts from mere wishful thinking to actionable steps, setting the stage for a deeper understanding of how societies can transform ideals into reality.
