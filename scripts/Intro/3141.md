## Introduction

In the shadowy streets and glittering speakeasies of Jazz Age New York, a new kind of detective work emerges—one that relies not on intuition or confession, but on the cold, precise language of chemistry. The Poisoner's Handbook: Murder and the Birth of Forensic Medicine in Jazz Age New York by Deborah Blum plunges into a world where poison is both a weapon and a puzzle, and where the boundaries between life and death are often determined by the skill of a handful of pioneering scientists. As the city pulses with energy and excess, a silent epidemic of mysterious deaths challenges the limits of law enforcement and medical knowledge, demanding innovation in the face of cunning criminality.

Amidst the chaos, the relentless pursuit of truth transforms the very nature of justice. The rise of forensic medicine, led by determined medical examiners and toxicologists, brings science to the forefront of criminal investigation, forever altering the way society confronts crime. Each case becomes a battle between those who wield poison and those who seek to unmask them, revealing the intricate dance between progress and peril. The story unfolds as a testament to human ingenuity and the enduring quest to bring order to a world teetering on the edge of chaos, setting the stage for a new era in the fight against invisible killers.
