## Introduction

The struggle for women’s suffrage in the United States unfolds as a tapestry of courage, resilience, and determination, woven together by countless individuals whose names rarely appear in history books. In Why They Marched: Untold Stories of the Women Who Fought for the Right to Vote by Susan Ware, the spotlight shifts to the lesser-known figures whose personal convictions and everyday actions fueled a movement that would reshape the nation. The fight for the right to vote was not a monolithic campaign led solely by famous leaders, but a collective effort powered by women from diverse backgrounds, each bringing her own story, motivations, and sacrifices to the cause.

Through the lens of these untold stories, the suffrage movement emerges as a vibrant, multifaceted struggle, marked by both unity and difference. The narrative journeys through the lives of activists, teachers, mothers, and workers, revealing how their unique experiences and local battles contributed to a national transformation. The movement’s complexity comes alive in the intersections of race, class, and geography, highlighting the ways in which ordinary women became extraordinary agents of change. As the threads of their stories intertwine, the broader significance of their fight becomes clear, inviting a deeper understanding of the ongoing quest for equality and justice.
