## Introduction

A world increasingly shaped by digital footprints and invisible surveillance raises profound questions about privacy, freedom, and the boundaries of state power. In Permanent Record, Edward Snowden’s journey unfolds against the backdrop of a society where every action, message, and search can be tracked, stored, and analyzed. The tension between personal liberty and national security pulses through the narrative, as the consequences of unchecked government oversight become impossible to ignore. The story is not just about technology, but about the moral choices that define an era—choices that ripple outward, affecting millions who may never realize how exposed their lives have become.

From the corridors of intelligence agencies to the global stage, the stakes grow ever higher as Snowden’s path reveals the inner workings of mass surveillance and the ethical dilemmas faced by those entrusted with immense power. The evolution from a young, idealistic technologist to a whistleblower willing to risk everything for the truth highlights the complexity of loyalty, conscience, and responsibility. As the implications of surveillance culture come into focus, the narrative invites reflection on the meaning of privacy, the cost of transparency, and the courage required to challenge the status quo. The journey through these revelations sets the stage for a deeper understanding of the digital age and its impact on individual autonomy.
