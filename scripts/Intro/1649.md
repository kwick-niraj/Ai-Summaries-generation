## Introduction

The collision of identity, race, and power reverberates through every aspect of life where colonial histories linger. In Black Skin, White Masks by Frantz Fanon, the psychological and social realities of Black existence in a world shaped by white supremacy come into sharp focus. The struggle to define oneself amid imposed stereotypes and the internalization of racism forms a complex web, where language, culture, and self-perception are all battlegrounds. The legacy of colonization is not just political or economic—it is deeply personal, shaping the very sense of self and belonging.

Navigating these tensions, the experience of alienation and the longing for recognition become central. The search for dignity and authenticity unfolds against a backdrop of systemic oppression, where the desire to be seen as fully human clashes with the weight of historical prejudice. Frantz Fanon’s exploration reveals how the wounds of colonialism are carried in the mind and body, influencing relationships, aspirations, and even dreams. The journey through these ideas invites a deeper understanding of liberation—not just as a political act, but as a profound transformation of consciousness and identity.
