## Introduction

Everyday decisions, beliefs, and memories often feel like products of careful reasoning and objective observation, yet beneath the surface, a complex web of cognitive biases and self-deceptions quietly shapes perception and behavior. The world of You Are Not So Smart by David McRaney delves into the fascinating landscape of human psychology, revealing how the mind routinely tricks itself into believing it is rational, consistent, and in control. From the subtle influence of confirmation bias to the surprising malleability of memory, the journey through these mental blind spots uncovers the hidden mechanisms that drive choices, opinions, and even the sense of self.

As the layers of self-delusion are peeled back, a clearer picture emerges of just how susceptible the mind is to error, illusion, and irrationality. Each cognitive quirk, from the Dunning-Kruger effect to the illusion of transparency, is not merely a curiosity but a powerful force shaping daily life and relationships. The exploration of these psychological phenomena invites a deeper understanding of why people so often misjudge situations, misremember events, and cling to flawed beliefs. With this awareness comes the opportunity to recognize personal limitations and approach the world with greater humility and curiosity, setting the stage for a more mindful and informed way of thinking.
