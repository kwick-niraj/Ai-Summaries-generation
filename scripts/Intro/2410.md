## Introduction

Uncertainty often shapes the fate of new products and innovations, leaving even the most seasoned leaders wondering why some ideas flourish while others falter. In Competing Against Luck, Clayton M. Christensen delves into the heart of this unpredictability, challenging the notion that success in business is merely a matter of chance. The concept of "Jobs to Be Done" emerges as a powerful lens, revealing that customers don't simply buy products—they "hire" them to fulfill specific needs and solve particular problems in their lives. This perspective reframes innovation as a disciplined process, where understanding the true motivations behind customer choices becomes the key to unlocking consistent growth.

By exploring real-world examples and dissecting the forces that drive consumer behavior, the narrative uncovers how organizations can move beyond guesswork and intuition. Patterns emerge, showing that when companies align their offerings with the actual jobs customers need done, they create products and services that resonate deeply and stand the test of time. The journey through these insights offers a roadmap for transforming innovation from a gamble into a repeatable, reliable strategy, setting the stage for a deeper exploration of how to compete—and win—against the unpredictable forces of luck.
