## Introduction

Long before Columbus set foot on the shores of the New World, the Americas thrived with civilizations rich in complexity, innovation, and diversity. The world of 1491: New Revelations of the Americas Before Columbus by Charles C. Mann invites a reimagining of pre-Columbian history, challenging the long-held assumptions about the indigenous peoples of North and South America. Far from being sparsely populated wildernesses, these continents were home to societies that engineered vast cities, developed sophisticated agricultural systems, and shaped their environments in ways that rivaled the achievements of their Old World counterparts.

As the layers of myth and misconception are peeled away, a vibrant tapestry of cultures emerges—one marked by ingenuity, adaptation, and resilience. The narrative journeys through the bustling markets of Tenochtitlan, the engineered landscapes of the Amazon, and the intricate alliances and rivalries that defined indigenous politics. With each revelation, the familiar story of the Americas transforms, offering a deeper appreciation for the ingenuity and agency of its first inhabitants. This exploration sets the stage for a profound shift in understanding, inviting a closer look at the true legacy of the Americas before European contact.
