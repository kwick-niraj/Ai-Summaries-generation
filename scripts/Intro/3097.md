## Introduction

Long before the rise of agriculture and the structures of modern society, human sexuality unfolded in ways that challenge today’s assumptions about monogamy, desire, and partnership. Sex at Dawn: The Prehistoric Origins of Modern Sexuality by Christopher Ryan delves into the evolutionary roots of human sexual behavior, questioning the conventional wisdom that lifelong monogamy is a natural or inevitable state. The exploration of ancient societies and primate relatives reveals a tapestry of intimacy, cooperation, and fluidity, suggesting that the patterns of love and attraction seen today are shaped by millennia of adaptation rather than rigid moral codes.

As the journey moves through anthropology, biology, and psychology, familiar beliefs about fidelity, jealousy, and gender roles are re-examined in the light of evidence from humanity’s deep past. The interplay between social structures and sexual expression emerges as a central theme, inviting a reconsideration of what it means to love and connect. With each insight, the boundaries of what is considered “normal” or “natural” begin to blur, opening the door to a more nuanced understanding of human relationships and the forces that have shaped them over time.
