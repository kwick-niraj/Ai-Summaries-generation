## Introduction

Everyday choices, from what to eat for lunch to how much to save for retirement, are shaped by subtle forces that often go unnoticed. The world of Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness by Richard H. Thaler reveals how human decision-making is influenced by cognitive biases, social pressures, and the way options are presented. Rather than acting as perfectly rational agents, people are swayed by habits, emotions, and the structure of their environments—sometimes to their benefit, but often to their detriment. The concept of "choice architecture" emerges as a powerful tool, suggesting that small changes in how choices are framed can lead to better outcomes without restricting freedom.

Within this landscape, the interplay between autonomy and guidance becomes central. By understanding the predictable ways in which people err, it becomes possible to design systems that gently steer individuals toward wiser decisions in areas like health, finances, and personal well-being. The journey through Thaler’s insights uncovers practical strategies for harnessing behavioral science to foster positive change, all while respecting individual liberty. As the exploration unfolds, the promise of a more thoughtful, supportive approach to everyday choices comes into focus.
