## Introduction

In the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina, the boundaries between heroism and suspicion blur, and the meaning of home is tested by forces both natural and human-made. Zeitoun by Dave Eggers follows the journey of Abdulrahman Zeitoun, a Syrian-American who chooses to stay behind in New Orleans to protect his property and help neighbors as the city floods. Amid the chaos, acts of compassion and resilience emerge, but so do the darker undercurrents of fear, prejudice, and institutional failure. The story unfolds against a backdrop where the American dream collides with the realities of disaster, and where the simple act of staying behind becomes a profound statement of faith, responsibility, and hope.

As the floodwaters rise, so do the stakes for Zeitoun and his family, who are separated by distance and uncertainty. The narrative weaves together the intimate details of daily survival with the larger questions of justice, identity, and belonging. Through the lens of one family's ordeal, the complexities of post-Katrina New Orleans come into sharp focus, revealing both the strength and fragility of community. The journey is not just about weathering a storm, but about confronting the unexpected consequences of disaster and the resilience required to endure them.
