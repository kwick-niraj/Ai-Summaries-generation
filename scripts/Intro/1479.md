## Introduction

A world shaped by privilege and tradition rarely expects its own to challenge the very foundations that support it. Yet, in the crucible of the Great Depression, Franklin Delano Roosevelt emerged as a paradox: a patrician who championed the common man, a scion of wealth who reimagined the social contract. Traitor to His Class by H.W. Brands delves into the complexities of Roosevelt’s life, tracing the journey from his gilded upbringing to the radical choices that defined his presidency. The tension between inherited status and a deepening empathy for the struggles of ordinary Americans forms the heart of this story, revealing how personal conviction can upend the expectations of birthright.

As the nation teetered on the brink of collapse, Roosevelt’s leadership redefined the possibilities of American democracy. His New Deal policies, bold and controversial, sought to restore hope and stability, while his political acumen navigated fierce opposition from both entrenched elites and populist critics. The narrative unfolds against the backdrop of global turmoil and domestic transformation, capturing the evolution of a leader who dared to betray his class for the promise of a more just society. Through the lens of Roosevelt’s life, the era’s sweeping changes come alive, inviting reflection on the enduring impact of courage, vision, and the willingness to defy one’s own origins.
