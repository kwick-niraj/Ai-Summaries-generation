## Introduction

A journey into the heart of memory and survival unfolds through the life of Cudjo Lewis, the last known survivor of the transatlantic slave trade, whose story pulses at the center of Barracoon: The Story of the Last Black Cargo by Zora Neale Hurston. The legacy of forced migration, cultural dislocation, and the enduring strength of the human spirit emerges as Cudjo recounts his capture in Africa, the harrowing Middle Passage, and the struggle to forge a new identity in a land that was never meant to be home. The echoes of ancestral loss and the search for belonging resonate through every recollection, revealing the personal cost of a history too often told in numbers rather than names.

Through Cudjo’s voice, the complexities of freedom, memory, and resilience are laid bare, inviting reflection on the meaning of home and the scars of displacement. The narrative weaves together oral history, cultural memory, and the intimate details of daily life, illuminating the ways in which trauma and hope coexist. Hurston’s attentive listening allows the rhythms of Cudjo’s speech and the weight of his experiences to shape a portrait that is both deeply individual and universally significant. The story transitions from the pain of captivity to the ongoing quest for dignity and connection, offering a powerful meditation on endurance and the reclamation of identity.
