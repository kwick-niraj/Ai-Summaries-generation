## Introduction

Hidden beneath the surface of everyday digital interactions, a vast trove of data quietly accumulates, capturing the unfiltered truths of human behavior. In a world where social masks are worn and polite fictions abound, the search for authenticity often leads to dead ends—except, perhaps, in the anonymous corners of the internet. Everybody Lies: Big Data, New Data, and What the Internet Can Tell Us About Who We Really Are by Seth Stephens-Davidowitz delves into the fascinating realm where search engines, social media, and online habits reveal what people truly think, desire, and fear. The digital footprints left behind—search queries, clicks, and posts—offer a candid, sometimes unsettling, portrait of society’s hidden anxieties and aspirations.

Patterns emerge from billions of data points, challenging assumptions about honesty, prejudice, sexuality, and even happiness. The analysis of this new data uncovers surprising truths about how people behave when no one is watching, exposing the gap between public personas and private realities. As the boundaries between public and private blur, the implications for understanding politics, economics, relationships, and self-perception become profound. The journey through these revelations invites a reconsideration of what is known about human nature, setting the stage for a deeper exploration of the insights and ethical dilemmas that arise when big data becomes a window into the soul.
