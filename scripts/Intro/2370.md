## Introduction

Hidden beneath the surface of everyday interactions, subtle patterns and unspoken rules shape the way people connect, compete, and communicate. Games People Play by Eric Berne delves into the fascinating world of social dynamics, revealing how much of human behavior is guided by psychological "games"—repetitive, predictable exchanges that often mask deeper motives and emotional needs. These games, ranging from the seemingly innocent to the quietly destructive, influence relationships at home, in the workplace, and among friends, often without conscious awareness.

Exploring the intricate dance of transactional analysis, the narrative uncovers the roles individuals unconsciously adopt and the scripts they follow in pursuit of validation, power, or intimacy. Each interaction becomes a window into the underlying drives that motivate choices and reactions, offering a new lens through which to view both conflict and connection. As the layers of these social games are peeled back, a clearer understanding emerges of how to break free from unhelpful patterns and foster more authentic, fulfilling relationships, setting the stage for deeper self-awareness and genuine change.
