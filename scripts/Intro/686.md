## Introduction

Longing, wonder, and the search for meaning shape the early years of a life, weaving together moments of delight and sorrow into a tapestry of self-discovery. In Surprised by Joy: The Shape of My Early Life, C.S. Lewis traces the arc of his formative experiences, from the innocence of childhood through the intellectual rigor of adolescence and into the awakening of spiritual curiosity. The persistent ache of “joy”—a fleeting, piercing sense of something beyond the ordinary—serves as both compass and mystery, guiding each step along the path. Through memories of family, school, friendships, and the books that shaped his imagination, the journey unfolds as a quest for understanding the elusive nature of happiness and the deeper truths that lie beneath the surface of daily life.

As the narrative moves through the landscapes of early twentieth-century England and the inner world of a sensitive, questioning mind, the interplay between reason and emotion, skepticism and faith, becomes ever more pronounced. Encounters with beauty, loss, and the challenges of belief gradually reveal the contours of a life shaped by longing and transformation. The persistent pursuit of joy—at once a personal yearning and a universal human experience—emerges as the thread connecting childhood dreams to adult convictions, inviting reflection on the ways in which the search for meaning can lead to unexpected revelations and profound change.
