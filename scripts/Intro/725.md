## Introduction

Civilizations rise with ambition, ingenuity, and the promise of progress, yet history reveals a recurring pattern of societies outpacing their own limits. In A Short History of Progress, Ronald Wright delves into the cycles of human advancement, exploring how innovation and expansion often sow the seeds of collapse. The pursuit of growth, while fueling remarkable achievements, can also blind societies to the warning signs of environmental strain and social imbalance. The echoes of vanished cultures—those that once flourished and then fell—linger as cautionary tales, urging a closer look at the choices that shape collective destiny.

Wright’s exploration moves through the ruins of past empires, from Sumer and Rome to Easter Island, tracing the common threads that connect their downfalls. The relentless drive for more—more land, more resources, more power—emerges as both a catalyst for greatness and a harbinger of disaster. As the modern world stands at a crossroads, the lessons embedded in these ancient stories become ever more urgent. The journey through humanity’s past offers not only a mirror for present-day ambitions but also a map of the pitfalls that lie ahead, inviting reflection on the true meaning of progress and the wisdom needed to sustain it.
