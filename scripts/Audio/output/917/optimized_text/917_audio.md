 Next, there’s **posture**. Sitting or standing with an open, relaxed posture says, “I’m here, I’m interested.” Leaning in a bit shows you’re engaged with what they’re saying. On the other hand, turning away or slouching might send the message that you’re bored or even defensive.
 
 Gestures matter, too. Using your hands to highlight what you’re saying, nodding along, or gently mirroring how the other person moves—all those little actions reinforce the feeling of being in sync.

 The brain is always looking for signs of “sameness,” and when it finds them, it feels safe and at home.
 
 There’s also the idea of **personal space**. 
 
 Everyone’s comfort zone is different, and it can depend a lot on culture or personality. Being aware of how close you stand or sit matters—a little sensitivity goes a long way in showing respect.
 
 Picture a job interview: someone walks in with confidence, gives a solid handshake, and keeps good eye contact. 
 
 Right away, they’re seen as confident and trustworthy, often before they even say a word. Those nonverbal cues set the whole tone for what comes next.
 
 So, here’s the key takeaway: body language is like your silent partner in every conversation.
 
 When you’re aware of the signals you send—and the ones you’re picking up—you can use mirroring and matching to build trust and connection, quickly and authentically.
