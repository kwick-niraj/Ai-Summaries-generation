 Write a purpose statement. Just a sentence or two that sums up what you want and why it matters.
 
 And break your big goals into small, daily actions that feel doable.
 
 When your habits are anchored in clear intentions and meaningful goals, the path forward is a whole lot clearer— and you’ll have the resilience to stick with it, even when life throws you a curveball. ## Chapter 10 Sustaining Momentum and Mastering the Habit Loop

**Chapter 10: Sustaining Momentum and Mastering the Habit Loop**
 
 So, here we are—the final step in building lasting change. It’s all about keeping your momentum going, and truly understanding something called the “habit loop.” If you picture it, the habit loop is just a simple circle—yet it’s powerful enough to shape nearly everything you do.
 
 Here’s how it works. First, there’s the **cue**: that little trigger that kicks things off. It could be waking up in the morning, feeling a bit stressed, or even seeing your running shoes by the door.
 
 Next comes the **routine**—the action itself. Maybe it’s stretching, writing in your journal, or simply pouring that first cup of coffee.
 
 And finally, there’s the **reward**. This is the good feeling or outcome you get—like feeling relaxed, satisfied, or just proud you followed through. That reward is what keeps pulling you back.
 
 So, how do you make the most of the habit loop? Start by spotting cues that already fit your day. Try hooking new habits onto things you do without thinking—brushing your teeth, making coffee, or turning off your alarm. When you link a new habit to something familiar, it naturally becomes part of your daily rhythm.
 
 Now, let’s talk about the routine itself. If a habit feels like a chore, you probably won’t stick with it. So, make it as enjoyable and easy as you can. Could you add some music? Invite a friend to join you? Or maybe move it to your favorite spot? The more you look forward to the routine, the faster it’ll become second nature.
 
 And don’t forget the reward. After you complete your habit, pause for a second. Notice how you feel. Let yourself enjoy that little boost—whether it’s a sense of calm, accomplishment, or simple satisfaction. The more you savor that reward, the more your brain will crave it next time, turning your habit into something automatic.
 
 Here are a few ways to keep your momentum strong:
 
 First, try stacking habits. Pair a new action with something you already do every day. It’s like building a chain that’s hard to break.
 
 Second, don’t be afraid to experiment with your cues and rewards. Maybe a different trigger works better, or you need a new way to celebrate your win.
 
 And finally, keep an eye on your progress—and celebrate every victory, no matter how small. Each step forward is worth noticing.
 
 Here’s a simple example: Let’s say you want to read more. You might put a book right on your pillow so you see it before bed—that’s your cue. You read a few pages as your routine. And the reward? You get to unwind and feel calm before sleep.
 
 By shaping your own habit loop, you’ll find it’s much easier to keep going—and even enjoy the process. Soon, those habits won’t be about willpower at all. They’ll just feel like a natural, enjoyable part of who you are.
