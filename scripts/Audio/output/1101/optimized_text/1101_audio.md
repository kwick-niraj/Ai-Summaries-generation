The camp’s rules were arbitrary and ever-changing. Punishments were handed out for the smallest infractions, and the threat of violence hung over every interaction. Yet, within this system, some prisoners found ways to adapt. Still, the cost of survival was high. Many found themselves making choices they never could have imagined—trading a friend’s trust for a piece of bread, or turning a blind eye to another’s suffering. The camp forced people to confront the limits of their own morality, and the line between right and wrong became blurred. Some managed to hold onto their principles, while others did what they had to in order to survive.

At night, the barracks were filled with the sounds of coughing, quiet weeping, and the restless shifting of bodies. Sleep brought little relief, as nightmares often replaced the horrors of the day.
