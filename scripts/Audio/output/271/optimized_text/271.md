Chapter 2 The Hidden World of Domestic Slavery

After the violence of abduction, the journey led to a new and unfamiliar world. The young girl, now separated from her family and everything familiar, was taken north—far from the Nuba Mountains and into the heart of a bustling city. Here, the rules were different, and the language spoken was not her own. The city’s noise and crowds were overwhelming, but what awaited her behind closed doors was even more disorienting.

She was handed over to a family who saw her not as a person, but as a possession. Her new life revolved around endless chores—cleaning, cooking, fetching water, and caring for children. From before sunrise until late at night, there was always more work to be done. Any mistake, no matter how small, was met with harsh words or physical punishment. There was no time for rest, no space for play, and no one to offer comfort.

The family’s children treated her with indifference or cruelty, mimicking the attitudes of their parents. She was not allowed to eat with them or share in their joys. Instead, she ate scraps in the kitchen, always aware that she was different, always reminded of her place. The sense of isolation was profound. Even in a house full of people, she felt utterly alone.

Most people do not realize how easily domestic slavery can be hidden. From the outside, the house looked like any other. Neighbors came and went, never questioning the presence of a silent, overworked girl in the background. The family maintained a respectable appearance, their secrets carefully guarded. In this way, the system of exploitation continued unchecked, protected by silence and indifference.

The psychological toll was immense. Over time, the girl began to doubt her own worth. She was told repeatedly that she was stupid, dirty, and unlovable. These words sank deep, eroding her confidence and making escape seem impossible. The physical exhaustion was matched by a sense of hopelessness, a belief that things would never change.

Yet, small acts of resistance persisted. Sometimes, she would steal a moment to remember her family, to recall the songs and stories of her childhood. These memories became a lifeline, a way to hold on to her sense of self. Occasionally, a kind word from a stranger or a fleeting glimpse of freedom would reignite her determination. It became clear that even in the most oppressive circumstances, the human spirit can find ways to endure.

The reality of modern slavery is often invisible to those who do not look closely. It hides in plain sight, protected by cultural norms and social hierarchies. Many fall into the habit of ignoring what makes them uncomfortable, choosing not to see the suffering of others. But for those trapped in this system, every day is a struggle for dignity and survival.
