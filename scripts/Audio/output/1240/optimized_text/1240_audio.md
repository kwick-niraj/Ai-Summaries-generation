## Chapter 1 Recognizing Gendered Barriers and Building Awareness

Let’s dive into Chapter 1: Recognizing Gendered Barriers and Building Awareness.
 
 Stepping into leadership as a woman today? It’s not just about skill or ambition—it’s about navigating a landscape filled with both obvious and hidden obstacles. 
 
 The journey really begins by seeing these barriers for what they are—not just as big-picture concepts, but as forces that shape your daily work, your opportunities, and even how you view yourself.
 
 **So, what does this landscape look like?** 
 
 In so many workplaces, gender bias isn’t always loud or in-your-face. Sometimes, it’s clear—like being passed over for a well-deserved promotion. But more often, it’s subtle: you’re interrupted in meetings, your ideas get credited to someone else, or you’re asked to pick up “office housework” that never leads to a raise or recognition.
 
 These moments might seem small on their own, but they add up—like carrying an invisible weight that slows you down.
 
 The first step? Awareness.
 
 Realizing these experiences aren’t just about you, and they aren’t signs of personal failure. They’re actually part of a much bigger pattern, woven into the culture of many workplaces. And once you see it, you can shift from doubting yourself to finding smart ways to move forward.
 
 Now, let’s talk about something called the “double bind.” 
 
 Women who aim for leadership often feel caught between being assertive and being likable—yet, somehow, being criticized for either trait if it’s seen as too strong or too soft. It’s a tough balancing act, and it can make you second-guess yourself or hold back.
 
 On top of that, there’s what’s known as “stereotype threat.” 
 
 It’s the worry that you’ll confirm negative stereotypes about women in leadership, and that fear alone can chip away at your confidence and performance.
 
 Here’s why understanding these dynamics really matters: 
 
 It’s like learning the rules of a game that wasn’t built for everyone. When you spot the patterns, you’re less likely to be blindsided—you can anticipate the challenges and choose how you want to respond.
 
 **Self-awareness** becomes your compass here. 
 
 It’s about checking in with yourself—reflecting on your strengths, your values, and what tends to push your buttons. When you know yourself well, it’s easier to sort out feedback that’ll help you grow from feedback that’s just plain biased.
 
 Plus, that clarity helps you bounce back from setbacks and keep going, even when things get tough.
 
 So, how do you build self-awareness in real life?
 
 Try setting aside a little time each week to think about your workplace experiences.
 
 Ask for honest feedback from people you trust—colleagues or mentors who really get you.
 
 And pay attention to patterns: How are you being treated? How does that compare to your peers?
 
 There’s also real power in naming and framing what you’re experiencing.
 
 When you call out things like microaggressions or double standards, it’s like switching on a light in a dark room. Suddenly, what was confusing or discouraging becomes something you can actually address.
 
 And when you see these moments as part of a bigger context, it’s easier not to blame yourself.
 
 For instance, let’s say your idea gets ignored in a meeting. Instead of thinking you did something wrong, you might recognize it as a common pattern—and then, maybe you follow up with a summary email or ask an ally to help amplify your voice next time.
 
 All of this—recognizing, naming, reframing—isn’t about giving up or getting cynical.
 
 It’s about understanding the lay of the land, so you’re better equipped to move ahead with clarity and purpose.
 
 When you see the terrain for what it is, you can start charting your own path—one that acknowledges the obstacles, but doesn’t let them define you.

## Chapter 2 Cultivating Resilience and a Growth Mindset

Now that we've explored the landscape of gendered barriers, let's talk about something even more personal—your inner resources. 
 
 Welcome to Chapter 2: Cultivating Resilience and a Growth Mindset.
 
 When you’re facing tough challenges or unexpected setbacks, what helps you keep going? Two powerful tools stand out: resilience and a growth mindset. These aren’t just buzzwords—they’re the heart of how you bounce back, learn, and stay motivated, even when things get tough.
 
 
 **Resilience as a Practice**
 
 Here’s the thing: resilience isn’t something you’re simply born with. It’s a skill you can build, just like learning to ride a bike. Imagine being able to recover from disappointments, adapt to sudden changes, and keep your eyes on your long-term goals—even if you hit a few bumps along the way. 
 
 In leadership, resilience is showing up and staying engaged, even when progress feels slow or you don’t always get the recognition you deserve.
 
 So, how do you actually build resilience? It often starts with how you see setbacks. Instead of seeing a missed opportunity as the end of the road, what if you could view it as a chance to learn and regroup? This way, disappointment stings less, and you keep moving forward.
 
 Want some practical ways to boost your resilience? Try setting up routines that help you recharge—maybe regular exercise, a bit of mindfulness, or even a creative hobby you love. 
 
 Set realistic goals for yourself and celebrate even the small wins. And remember, you don’t have to go it alone—reaching out to friends, mentors, or your professional network can make all the difference when times are tough.

 
 **Embracing a Growth Mindset**
 
 Have you ever thought, “I’m just not good at this”? That’s a fixed mindset talking. But a growth mindset—now that’s different. It’s believing that you can get better with effort, learning, and persistence.
 
 For women stepping into leadership, this is especially important. Instead of seeing challenges as threats, you start to see them as chances to stretch, grow, and prove to yourself what you’re capable of.
 
 This shift is especially powerful in spaces where women might be few and far between, or where others might doubt your leadership. By focusing on learning and steady progress—not just perfection—you’ll feel more comfortable taking risks, asking for feedback, and getting back up when you make mistakes.
 
 So what does a growth mindset look like day to day? It’s seeing criticism not as a personal attack, but as helpful information you can use to get better. It’s choosing to tackle new challenges, even if they make you nervous. And it’s knowing that true mastery comes from sticking with it and practicing, over and over.

 
 **The Role of Self-Compassion**
 
 Here’s something we often overlook: being kind to ourselves. Self-compassion means showing yourself the same understanding and encouragement you’d offer a friend, especially when things get hard.
 
 It’s about noticing that inner critic and gently steering your thoughts toward support instead of harsh judgment. 
 
 Remind yourself that setbacks are part of any ambitious path. And when you connect with others who’ve faced similar struggles, you’ll feel less alone—and a lot more resilient.

 
 **Turning Setbacks into Stepping Stones**
 
 The truth is, every leader runs into obstacles. What sets successful people apart isn’t a life free of setbacks, but the ability to turn those tough moments into fuel for growth. 
 
 Picture this: a woman gets passed over for a leadership role. Instead of giving up, she uses that experience to spot where she can grow, searches out specific training, and gets ready to make an even stronger case next time.
 
 When you blend resilience, a growth mindset, and a little self-compassion, you turn challenges into stepping stones. You’ll not only boost your own well-being, but also build the confidence you need to chase your most ambitious goals.

## Chapter 3 Building and Leveraging Supportive Networks

**Chapter 3: Building and Leveraging Supportive Networks**
 
 Once you’ve built up a resilient mindset, there’s another crucial piece of the puzzle—building the kind of relationships that lift you up, guide you, and open new doors. Let’s face it, nobody gets ahead completely on their own. Your networks and mentors? They’re like the scaffolding that helps you reach higher and keeps you steady.
 
 **The Value of Networks**
 
 Think of your professional network as more than just a long list of business cards or LinkedIn connections. It’s really an ecosystem—a place where support, information, and opportunities all flow both ways. For women aiming for leadership, these networks become even more important. They can help break down barriers, connect you to resources, and get you noticed by the right people.
 
 Networks come in all shapes and sizes. You’ve got your peer network, those colleagues who cheer you on, share what they’ve learned, and really get what you’re going through. Then there are mentors—people who’ve walked the path before you and are generous with their guidance and feedback. And don’t forget about sponsors. These are senior leaders who will actually advocate for you, putting your name forward for opportunities you might not even know exist yet.
 
 Each of these relationships plays a different role, and together, they create a strong web of support—one that helps you navigate tricky situations and keep moving forward.
 
 **Finding and Nurturing Mentors**
 
 Let’s talk about mentors. A great mentor is like having a trusted guide—someone who offers wisdom, encouragement, and sometimes the honest feedback you need to hear. They can point out blind spots, help you think through tough decisions, and even suggest strategies for overcoming obstacles.
 
 So, how do you find a mentor? Start by looking for people whose values or leadership style really resonate with you. When you approach them, come with a specific question or ask for advice on something you’re genuinely working through. That makes the interaction meaningful—and it can be the start of an ongoing relationship. And remember, mentorship is a two-way street. Show your appreciation, follow up on their advice, and, when you can, offer support back. That reciprocity strengthens the connection.
 
 Mentorship can happen in all sorts of ways. Maybe it’s through a formal company program, or perhaps it’s an informal relationship that grows out of a shared interest or project. Sometimes, even peer mentoring—when you and a colleague at a similar stage encourage each other—can make a huge difference.
 
 **The Power of Sponsorship**
 
 Now, while mentors help you grow, sponsors actually use their influence to create new opportunities for you. Imagine a senior leader who recommends you for a high-profile project, advocates for your promotion, or introduces you to key decision-makers. That’s sponsorship in action—and for women, especially in environments where they’re underrepresented, it can be a game changer.
 
 Building a sponsorship relationship takes a little groundwork. Show you’re reliable, take initiative, and deliver results. And make sure your achievements—and your ambitions—are visible. After all, sponsors need to know what you’re aiming for if they’re going to help you get there.
 
 **Expanding and Diversifying Networks**
 
 The most effective networks aren’t just big—they’re diverse. When you connect with people from different departments, industries, and backgrounds, you get fresh perspectives, access to new information, and a network that’s more resilient when things change.
 
 So, how do you grow your network? Try attending industry events or workshops, join employee resource groups or professional associations, or volunteer for cross-functional projects. And don’t forget to nurture the relationships you already have—check in, celebrate others’ successes, and offer help when you can. Networks really thrive on genuine connection and giving back.
 
 **Navigating the Challenges of Networking**
 
 If the idea of networking makes you a bit uncomfortable, you’re not alone. But what if you thought of it as simply building relationships—focused on mutual support and growth? Try setting small, achievable goals, like reaching out to one new contact each month. Little by little, you’ll build confidence and momentum.
 
 And if you’re in a place where women are underrepresented, consider forming an affinity group or an informal circle. These spaces can be powerful for sharing experiences, learning new strategies, and even driving collective change.
 
 **Networks as Catalysts for Advancement**
 
 At the end of the day, strong networks do more than just support you. They amplify your visibility, open doors to new opportunities, and create a real sense of belonging. For women who are aspiring to lead, investing in these relationships isn’t a distraction—it’s actually a key strategy for moving forward and achieving your goals.

## Chapter 4 Mastering Self-Advocacy and Personal Branding

Let’s dive into Chapter 4: **Mastering Self-Advocacy and Personal Branding**.
 
 Now that you’ve built a foundation of self-awareness, resilience, and a supportive network, it’s time to take the next big step—learning how to advocate for yourself and shape a professional identity that truly stands out.
 
 You know, self-advocacy and personal branding aren’t just about promoting yourself for the sake of it. It’s really about making sure your hard work gets noticed, your voice is heard, and your goals are crystal clear to the people who can help you reach them.
 

 **The Art of Self-Advocacy**
 
 Self-advocacy means speaking up for your needs, your interests, and your achievements. It’s making sure your contributions are seen, that you get the credit you deserve, and that your ambitions aren’t kept a secret. 
 
 For many women, this can feel a bit uncomfortable—especially in places where modesty is valued over assertiveness. But here’s something worth remembering: research has shown that those who advocate for themselves are more likely to get noticed for promotions and leadership roles.
 
 So, how can you practice self-advocacy in a way that feels authentic? Try preparing clear and simple summaries of your accomplishments and the impact you’ve made. Practice being assertive—but always respectful—when you speak up in meetings or during performance reviews. And don’t forget to set up regular check-ins with your manager or mentor to talk about your progress and your goals.
 

 **Navigating the Visibility Paradox**
 
 There’s a tricky balance here—being too visible can sometimes draw unwanted attention or criticism, while keeping your head down might mean your efforts go unnoticed. So, what do you do?
 
 The key is strategic visibility. That means choosing the right moments to highlight your achievements and finding allies who can help amplify your voice. For instance, you might share your successes as part of a team accomplishment, showing both assertiveness and collaboration. Or, you could volunteer to lead projects or present your work—those are great ways to stand out, while staying aligned with your organization’s values.
 

 **Crafting a Personal Brand**
 
 Let’s talk about personal branding. It’s really just about defining and sharing what makes you unique in your professional life. What do you stand for? What special strengths do you bring to the table? And how do others see you?
 
 A strong personal brand is authentic, consistent, and tied directly to your career goals. It helps people understand what you’re all about, and why you’re such a valuable asset.
 
 To get started, reflect on your core values, your passions, and the areas where you really shine. Ask colleagues for honest feedback about your strengths and reputation. Then, pull it all together into a concise narrative—one that links your past experiences to your future aspirations. 
 
 This story isn’t just for your resume. You can use it in your online profiles, when networking, or in interviews and performance reviews.
 

 **Leveraging Communication Skills**
 
 None of this works without strong communication. It’s not just about talking or writing clearly—it’s also about listening, picking up on the mood in the room, and tailoring your message for whoever you’re speaking with.
 
 Some tips? Prepare your talking points before those important conversations. Practice active listening to build trust and rapport. And use storytelling to make your achievements stick in people’s minds.
 

 **Overcoming Internal and External Barriers**
 
 Of course, there are challenges along the way. Sometimes, internal doubts—like imposter feelings or the fear of seeming boastful—can hold you back. But remember, these feelings are common and don’t reflect your true abilities. 
 
 On the outside, you might face biased feedback or a lack of recognition. That’s when persistence—and maybe a little creativity—can help. For example, you could seek out allies or make a habit of documenting your contributions.
 

 **The Impact of Self-Advocacy and Branding**
 
 When you learn to advocate for yourself and intentionally shape your professional identity, you increase your influence, unlock new opportunities, and inspire others to do the same. These skills go way beyond personal success—they help create workplaces that are more inclusive and equitable for everyone.
 
 So, as you move forward, remember that mastering self-advocacy and personal branding isn’t just about getting ahead. It’s about making sure your story is heard, your value is recognized, and your future is in your own hands.

Maybe you tweak how you communicate with different teams or pick up new skills as your organization shifts. The trick is to stay centered in your values, even as your strategies shift.

Keep checking in on your goals, too, so they stay fresh and exciting.

 Aligning authenticity with strategic planning isn’t something you do just once—it’s an ongoing journey. You’ll need to pause and reflect often, stay open to change, and sometimes take risks to chase after what’s truly meaningful to you.
 
 By combining self-awareness, resilience, strong connections, self-advocacy, and intentional planning, you can carve out a path that’s not only successful, but also deeply fulfilling.

## Chapter 6 Navigating Organizational Politics with Integrity

**Chapter Six: Navigating Organizational Politics with Integrity**
 
 As you move up into higher levels of leadership, things don’t just get more exciting—they also get a whole lot more complicated. Suddenly, you’re dealing with unspoken rules, shifting alliances, and a whole maze of invisible networks. 
 
 But here’s the thing—navigating organizational politics isn’t about playing games or putting yourself first. It’s about understanding how things really work behind the scenes, so you can advocate for yourself and your team, all while staying true to who you are.
 
 **Understanding the Political Landscape**
 
 Every organization has its own unique culture, its history, and a set of unwritten rules. Getting a feel for who actually holds influence, how decisions really get made, and where the informal power sits? That’s key. 
 
 When you know the lay of the land, you can spot challenges before they hit, find your allies, and steer clear of common traps.
 
 So, how do you start mapping out this political terrain? Try observing who gets consulted when big decisions are on the table, or which teams always seem to be in the loop on high-profile projects. 
 
 Pay attention to those hallway conversations or casual lunches where real talk happens. 
 
 Remember, this isn’t about gossip. It’s about tuning in to those subtle currents beneath the surface—almost like reading the wind and tides before you set sail.
 
 **Building Credibility and Trust**
 
 In the world of organizational politics, credibility is your currency. You earn it by showing up, following through, and acting ethically—even when nobody’s watching. 
 
 If you’re someone who delivers results, keeps promises, and treats people with respect, you’ll quickly become someone others trust—and include in the conversations that matter.
 
 This isn’t about grand gestures. Sometimes, it’s as simple as keeping your word on the small stuff, admitting when you’ve made a mistake, and being generous with credit when things go well. 
 
 All these little things build a rock-solid foundation of trust, which is what you’ll need when you hit those inevitable bumps in the road.
 
 **Strategic Relationship-Building**
 
 Here’s something a lot of people overlook: building relationships isn’t just about networking with the folks at the top. 
 
 True influence comes from connecting across every level—the peers, the folks who report to you, even the people who keep things running behind the scenes. 
 
 These connections give you access to real information, fresh perspectives, and, let’s be honest, some much-needed support when things get tricky.
 
 For example, you might schedule regular catch-ups with people from other departments, volunteer for teams that bring different groups together, or offer your help with no strings attached. 
 
 Over time, you’ll weave a web of mutual support that you can lean on when you need to navigate tough situations.
 
 **Advocating for Self and Others**
 
 Navigating politics isn’t just about your own path. It’s also about standing up for your team, pushing for fairness, and making sure opportunities are opened up for everyone. 
 
 Maybe you use your seat at the table to shine a light on someone’s hard work, or you’re the one to speak up when a decision doesn’t feel transparent or fair. 
 
 Yes, it can feel risky. But these small acts? They can slowly change the culture and open new doors—not just for you, but for others too.
 
 **Managing Conflict with Grace**
 
 Let’s face it—conflict is just part of the deal, especially as you climb the ladder. 
 
 The real skill is handling it with grace. That means tackling problems head-on, really listening to different viewpoints, and looking for solutions that respect everyone involved. 
 
 Sometimes, you’ll need to stand your ground. Other times, it’s about finding that middle path.
 
 Try preparing for tough conversations by getting clear on what you want to achieve, using neutral language to describe what’s going on, and focusing on interests rather than getting stuck on positions. 
 
 When you see conflict as a chance to grow and understand others better, you not only build stronger relationships, but you also earn a reputation for fairness.
 
 **Staying True to Values**
 
 Above all, the most effective political navigators are the ones who stick to their values. 
 
 That might mean passing up a shortcut or saying no to something that doesn’t feel right—even when it’s tempting. 
 
 And if you ever find yourself in a situation that goes against what you truly believe, don’t be afraid to walk away.
 
 So, here’s the key takeaway: Navigating organizational politics with integrity is about understanding how things work, building trust, and using your influence to make positive change. 
 
 It’s a skill you can learn—and keep getting better at. And honestly? It’s essential if you want to lead with impact and stay true to yourself.

## Chapter 7 Sustaining Leadership Presence and Influence

Let’s dive into Chapter 7: Sustaining Leadership Presence and Influence.
 
 You know, leadership presence isn’t just about having confidence or a magnetic personality. It’s really about inspiring trust, commanding attention when it matters, and making people believe in what’s possible. And to keep that presence strong over time? Well, that takes ongoing self-awareness, adaptability, and a real commitment to growing as a person.
 
 So, what exactly is leadership presence? Think of it as a mix of gravitas, strong communication, and being authentically you. It’s the kind of quality that makes others tune in, feel genuinely heard, and get behind your vision. And here’s something a lot of people get wrong: it’s not about being the loudest in the room. True presence comes from projecting calm, clarity, and real conviction.
 
 There are a few key ingredients to this:
 
 Staying composed, even when the pressure’s on. Speaking clearly, with purpose. Showing empathy and really listening. And yes, projecting confidence—but never crossing into arrogance. The good news? These are skills you can absolutely build, just like working out a muscle—through practice and honest feedback.
 
 Now, let’s talk about emotional intelligence, or EQ. It’s that ability to recognize and manage your own emotions—and to pick up on what others are feeling, too. Leaders with high EQ? They’re the ones who form strong relationships, smooth over conflicts, and keep teams motivated.
 
 Want to build your emotional intelligence? Start by checking in with yourself—notice your moods and what triggers them. Practice empathy by really trying to see things from someone else’s perspective. And when stress creeps in, try mindfulness or other ways to stay steady. If you can do this, you’ll navigate the ups and downs of organizational life with more ease, and you’ll inspire trust in others along the way.
 
 Let’s shift to communicating vision and values. If you want to sustain your influence, you need to paint a clear picture of where you’re headed—and connect that vision to what matters most to your team. It’s not just about setting a direction; it’s about making people want to join you on the journey.
 
 One of the best ways to do this? Use stories and real-life examples. That helps make big ideas feel real and relatable. Also, link the organization’s goals to what motivates each person. And remember, consistency is key—repeat those core messages across different settings. When people understand the “why” behind your decisions and feel connected to a bigger purpose, they’re far more likely to put their hearts into your mission.
 
 Now, think about how leaders act when things get tough. People are watching closely—especially during uncertain times. If you can show resilience, stay calm, adapt as things change, and keep hope alive, your team will follow your lead. Imagine a leader during a big company restructuring: if they communicate openly, admit what’s uncertain, and focus on the opportunities for growth, that can really help ease anxiety and keep everyone motivated.
 
 Creating a feedback-rich environment is another big part of sustaining your presence and influence. If you make feedback a natural, everyday thing—asking for it, responding with gratitude, and encouraging open conversations—you’re setting everyone up for continuous improvement.
 
 So, how do you do it? Ask your team and your peers for their thoughts. When you get feedback, listen openly and show you’re willing to learn. And when you give feedback, make it specific and supportive. This not only sharpens your own skills but also helps others grow and shine.
 
 One more thing you really can’t ignore: guarding against burnout. Leadership presence won’t last if you’re running on empty. Burnout chips away at confidence, creativity, and your overall effectiveness. So, it’s crucial to prioritize self-care, set healthy boundaries, and reach out for support when you need it.
 
 Simple ways to keep burnout at bay? Schedule regular breaks, and don’t be afraid to delegate—trust others to help carry the load. Stay connected with peers or mentors who can offer encouragement and perspective. By taking care of yourself, you’ll be in a much better place to care for your team and keep your leadership impact strong over the long haul.

## Chapter 8 Championing Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion

Let's talk about something that's truly shaping the future of work—**championing diversity, equity, and inclusion.** As women step into leadership roles, they're in a unique spot to drive real change, not just because it's the right thing to do, but because it actually makes organizations stronger and more innovative.
 
 So, why does inclusion matter so much for business? Well, think about it—when you've got a team made up of different backgrounds and viewpoints, you're naturally going to see more creative ideas and better problem-solving. Research backs this up again and again: diverse teams just perform better. Plus, workplaces that truly value inclusion attract top talent, keep people engaged, and build a reputation as somewhere people actually want to work.
 
 But here's the thing—barriers to inclusion still pop up, even when we think we've made progress. Sometimes it's unconscious bias in hiring or promotions. Other times, it's a lack of access to important projects or those informal networks where big decisions get made. And then there are the subtle things—like microaggressions or feeling left out of the conversation.
 
 Spotting these patterns is the first step. Once you see them, you can start to break them down.
 
 So, how do you actually create a more inclusive environment? It starts with making sure every voice is heard and valued. That might mean changing up team structures or simply paying closer attention in meetings. For example, try rotating who leads your next meeting—give everyone a chance to be in the spotlight. Or, when hiring, use blind resume reviews to focus on talent, not unconscious bias.
 
 And don’t forget about setting the tone for respectful communication. Sometimes, just inviting quieter team members to share their thoughts can make a world of difference.
 
 Now, let's talk about equity. Equity takes things a step further than equality by recognizing that not everyone starts from the same place. Leaders who champion equity look out for systemic issues—like pay gaps or uneven access to development opportunities. They review compensation data, support mentorships for underrepresented groups, and back policies that help everyone balance work and life—especially when family responsibilities come into play.
 
 Using your influence as a leader goes even further. You’re not just shaping your own team; you’re helping shift the whole organization. Maybe you join a diversity council, lead a training, or advocate for supplier diversity. You might even partner with community groups to widen your talent pipeline.
 
 But at the end of the day, the most powerful way to champion diversity and inclusion is by leading by example. That means staying open to feedback, admitting when you don’t have all the answers, and making it a habit to seek out different perspectives—even when it challenges your own thinking.
 
 Inclusive leaders recognize that differences aren’t just to be tolerated—they’re a source of strength and fresh ideas. By showing humility, curiosity, and a willingness to grow, women leaders can inspire everyone around them to help build workplaces where everyone thrives.

## Chapter 9 Mentoring and Sponsoring the Next Generation

Let’s dive into Chapter 9: **Mentoring and Sponsoring the Next Generation**.

 

 You know, true leadership isn’t just about moving yourself forward—it’s about lifting up others as you go. When you mentor or sponsor someone, especially women and folks from underrepresented groups, you’re not just helping them succeed. You’re sparking a ripple effect that creates real, lasting change.

 

 **The Ripple Effect of Mentorship**

 Think about this: mentorship is one of the best ways to develop new talent, build real confidence, and open doors that might’ve stayed closed. When experienced leaders take the time to invest in others, they help break down barriers and speed up progress for everyone coming up behind them.

 

 What do mentors actually do? Well, they offer guidance on how to navigate the ins and outs of workplace culture and politics.
