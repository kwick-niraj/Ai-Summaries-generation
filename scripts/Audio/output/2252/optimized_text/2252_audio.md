 Think of bootstrapping like tending a garden with just the tools and seeds you already own.  There are some key principles to keep in mind when you’re bootstrapping:

 Second, **prioritize revenue.** Bring in income early, even if it means starting with consulting, freelancing, or small projects. Every dollar counts.
 
 Third, **manage your costs.** Keep expenses low. Avoid unnecessary hires or fancy overhead. Spend only where it truly matters.
 
 And finally, **reinvest wisely.** Use your profits to fund the next phase of growth—instead of relying on loans or chasing investors.
 
 Bootstrapping also changes how you handle risk. Instead of betting the farm on explosive, high-stakes growth, you’re focusing on steady progress—learning as you go, and building confidence with each step forward. This approach can ease stress, and it actually boosts your chance of long-term success.

With profits in hand, the founder slowly builds a small team and the company expands—at a pace that’s right for them.
