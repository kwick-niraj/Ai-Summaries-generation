## Chapter 2 The Box: How Justifying Ourselves Distorts Reality

Instead of acting on that genuine urge to help or connect, you hold back. And to make your choice feel okay, you begin to see the other person as less deserving, maybe less considerate, or even as the source of your own irritation.  Over time, those stories become the lens through which you see everything. Think about a manager who once loved a team member’s creativity, but now only notices their mistakes. Or a friend who used to enjoy someone’s company, but now just finds them annoying. The more we justify our own actions, the more warped our view of others becomes.
 
 And this self-justification? It doesn’t just stay in our heads. It spills into every conversation and interaction. When you’re in the box, your words and actions might carry a slight edge—maybe a hint of impatience, a bit of criticism, or just plain indifference. People pick up on it, even if you don’t say anything directly. They react—maybe by getting defensive, or pulling away. The cycle keeps going, with each person feeling even more justified in their own position.
 
 What’s wild is how sneaky this pattern is. Most of us don’t even realize when we’re in the box. We think our view is totally objective, our frustrations make sense, and that other people really are the problem. Rarely do we stop to wonder if our own mindset is shaping the very issues we’re struggling with.
 
 In organizations, this can quietly eat away at trust and teamwork. Leaders who see their teams as lazy or uncommitted often end up creating the very disengagement they’re worried about. Employees who feel undervalued just stop trying. The whole workplace culture shifts—blame and defensiveness become the norm, and everyone’s convinced they’re doing their best in tough circumstances.
 
 The box isn’t just a metaphor—it’s something you feel. It’s that sense of being stuck, seeing the same problems come up again and again, and wondering why nothing changes. 
 
 But as long as we’re focused on justifying ourselves and blaming others, we stay trapped. The box holds tight, and the cycle continues.
