## Chapter 2 How Language and Communication Shape Professional Perceptions

**Chapter Two: How Language and Communication Shape Professional Perceptions**
 
 Once you spot those hidden rules at work, it’s only natural to start wondering about the role language and communication play in how women are seen professionally. 
 
 Think about it—the words you choose, the tone you use, even the way you share your ideas... all of that can totally change how credible or authoritative you come across.
 
 Language isn’t just about getting a message across. It’s a signal. It shows your confidence, your competence, and what you really mean. 
 
 Sometimes, just a slight tweak in how you phrase something can either boost your message or quietly take it down a notch. 
 
 For example, have you ever started a suggestion with, “I’m not sure, but…” or “This might be a silly idea, but…”? 
 
 It might feel polite or humble, but honestly, it can make what you say seem less valuable—even if your idea is solid. 
 
 Most of us do this to avoid sounding arrogant or to soften disagreement. But here’s the catch: it can actually make you seem unsure of yourself, or lacking in conviction.
 
 There’s another thing to watch out for—using overly polite or apologetic language. 
 
 When you apologize for tiny things, or just for speaking up, it can quietly chip away at your authority. 
 
 Instead, being clear and direct is more likely to show you’re confident and decisive.
 
 And it’s not just about what you say. Body language matters, too.
 
 The way you sit or stand, the eye contact you make, even your gestures—all of these can either back up your words or send a mixed message.
 
 If you avoid eye contact, speak softly, or use hesitant gestures, even the best ideas might not land as powerfully. 
 
 But when you stand tall, look people in the eye, and use gestures that fit your message, you naturally come across as more confident—and people pay attention.
 
 Picture this: two colleagues are in a meeting, both sharing similar ideas. One speaks clearly, keeps steady eye contact, and uses assertive language. The other hedges her statements, looks down, and fidgets. 
 
 Even if both ideas are great, which one do you think will be remembered and taken seriously? That’s the power of communication style.
 
 So, what can you do to shift your communication patterns?
 
 Here are a few strategies to try:
 
 First, swap out tentative language for clear, direct statements. 
 
 Instead of saying, “I think maybe we could try…,” try, “I recommend we try…”
 
 Second, hold back on unnecessary apologies. Save “I’m sorry” for when it really counts—not just for sharing your opinion or taking up a bit of time.
 
 Third, use confident body language. Sit or stand upright, make eye contact, and gesture in ways that match what you’re saying.
 
 And finally, keep your pace and volume steady. 
 
 Resist the urge to rush or let your sentences trail off.
 
 Now, if all of this feels a little awkward at first, that’s totally normal—especially if you’re used to minimizing your presence. 
 
 But with practice, it gets easier. And over time, these changes can make a real difference in how credible you seem at work.
 
 One more thing: being assertive doesn’t mean being aggressive or dismissive. 
 
 The goal is to share your ideas and needs clearly, while still showing respect for others. 
 
 That’s the sweet spot for building trust and influence at work.
 
 So, when you become more intentional about your language and nonverbal cues, you start to reshape how others see your competence and leadership potential. 
 
 And that opens up a whole new world of opportunities and recognition.
