All of this added up to a system that locks up millions—disproportionately Black and brown people. But instead, entire communities got labeled as dangerous, left carrying the heavy weight of suspicion.

Or a mother, waiting for hours to see her son in prison—her hope rubbing up against exhaustion. Homelessness and mental illness get treated as crimes, not as issues that deserve compassion. It’s like the boundaries of the prison just keep expanding—moving into our homes, our schools, our streets.

 

 But here’s the thing—even with all this machinery, resistance is always there. Families come together to support loved ones behind bars. Activists push back, demanding an end to solitary confinement, to cash bail, to the endless cycle of punishment. The call for abolition—getting rid of prisons, of police, of the systems that make them seem necessary—it’s getting louder. And it isn’t just about tearing down walls. It’s about dreaming up a world where safety is built on care, not control.

 

 The fight against the carceral state isn’t simple. It means facing up to the fears and assumptions that make punishment feel like the only option. It means asking some tough questions about what justice really is, and who actually gets to decide. But the more people begin to see how all these things are connected—from Ferguson to Palestine, from prisons to poverty—the more real the possibility of change becomes.
