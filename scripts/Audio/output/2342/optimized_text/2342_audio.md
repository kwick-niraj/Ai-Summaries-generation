## Introduction

Welcome to a journey where we're invited to pause and really consider something incredible—the joy of God Himself. 
 
 Imagine, at the very center of everything, there’s a God who takes delight, not in a distant or impersonal way, but with a happiness that’s completely woven into all of creation. This isn’t just about duty or obligation. 
 
 It’s about discovering the beauty and deep satisfaction that come from God’s own enjoyment of being who He is—and how that joy spills over into the world around us.
 
 As we explore the pages of **The Pleasures of God** by John Piper together, get ready to see how God’s delight shapes everything—from His love for His Son, to His acts of mercy, justice, and grace.
 
 What if understanding what brings God joy could actually transform the way we see worship, obedience, and even our own search for happiness?
 
 Through these reflections, we’re invited into a fresh perspective—a chance to experience how the joy at the heart of God can lead us to deeper fulfillment and a richer connection with the very source of all true pleasure.
 
 So, let's set out together and discover what it really means to find delight in the One who delights in Himself—and in us.

## Chapter 1 Discovering What Brings God Joy

**Chapter 1: Discovering What Brings God Joy**
 
 Have you ever noticed how, in a quiet moment—maybe while you’re sipping coffee in the kitchen, or just watching someone you love laugh—you find yourself wondering, what really makes another person happy?
 
 It’s such a simple question, but it lingers. What brings real delight?
 
 Now, imagine turning that question toward God. Not just thinking about what makes people smile, but asking, what actually stirs joy in the heart of the One who created everything? That’s where this journey really starts. 
 
 And it doesn’t begin with rules or checklists—it starts with a longing to know what makes God glad.
 
 Most folks, when they picture God, see a distant ruler—or maybe a judge keeping score. But the story of God’s joy is so much more than that. It’s about a God who isn’t just powerful or wise, but deeply happy. 
 
 He delights in Himself, in His Son, and in everything He does.
 
 This isn’t just some cold, abstract idea. It’s the heartbeat of the universe—the source behind every true and lasting joy we ever feel.
 
 You know, for a lot of people, the search for happiness can feel endless. Maybe it’s a young professional, exhausted from another long week, quietly hoping the next promotion will finally bring contentment. Or a parent, wishing that their child’s success might fill the emptiness inside. 
 
 Even during celebrations, there’s sometimes this quiet ache, isn’t there? The world gives us so many ways to chase happiness, but it seems like hardly anyone finds it for long.
 
 But what if the answer isn’t about chasing our own pleasures? 
 
 What if it’s about discovering what brings pleasure to God? 
 
 That one question changes everything. It shifts our focus from ourselves to something so much greater. 
 
 It invites us to look past our own desires—and see the world through God’s eyes.
 
 The story of God’s joy actually begins with Himself. 
 
 Before there were stars, or people, or even time itself—God was already full of delight. He didn’t create the world because He was lonely or needed anything. 
 
 He was already complete. Already overflowing with happiness.
 
 Now, that’s tough for us to imagine, especially when our own happiness can feel so fragile and fleeting. 
 
 But for God, joy isn’t something He finds outside Himself. 
 
 It’s just who He is.
 
 This truth can really shake up old assumptions. 
 
 Many of us grew up thinking God’s main concern is our behavior—rewarding the good and punishing the bad. 
 
 But the deeper story is about God’s own gladness. 
 
 He delights in His own glory, in the beauty and perfection of His character.
 
 And that’s not selfishness—it’s actually the foundation of everything that’s good and beautiful. 
 
 If God were to delight in anything less than Himself, He’d be settling for something unworthy.
 
 And there’s a quiet freedom in realizing that God’s happiness doesn’t depend on us. 
 
 He’s not needy or insecure. 
 
 His joy is steady, unshakable, rooted in His own greatness.
 
 So this invitation to know God? 
 
 It’s not some heavy burden. 
 
 It’s a gift—an open door to share in the joy that’s always existed, long before any of us were born.
 
 As we keep exploring, you’ll see that understanding what pleases God isn’t just a theology lesson. 
 
 It’s actually the key to real worship, to a happiness that lasts, and to a life that truly makes sense. 
 
 When we finally see God for who He really is—full of joy, delighting in Himself and all He creates—our own hearts start to change. 
 
 Worship stops being a duty and becomes a natural, joyful response to the beauty and gladness of God.

## Chapter 2 God’s Joy in His Own Glory

**Chapter Two: God’s Joy in His Own Glory**
 
 Picture this—a craftsman, alone in his workshop, hands moving carefully as he shapes a piece of wood. He steps back, not to brag, but just to soak in the beauty of what he’s created. There’s a quiet kind of joy in that moment, right? The simple pleasure of seeing something good and true come to life.
 
 Now, take that feeling and stretch it deeper—far beyond anything we could ever experience. That’s a glimpse of how God finds joy in the beauty and perfection of His own character—in His glory.
 
 The word “glory” can sound distant, maybe even a little intimidating. You might picture blinding lights, grand ceremonies, or standards that feel way out of reach. But at its core, glory is really about worth and beauty. It’s what makes something, or someone, truly valuable.
 
 So when God delights in His own glory, He’s celebrating the fullness of who He is—perfect, wise, loving, and just. 
 
 Honestly, this idea can feel a bit strange at first. We’re taught to steer clear of pride and self-centeredness. But here’s something to think about: for God, delighting in Himself isn’t arrogance—it’s the only response that makes sense to infinite goodness. If God ever put anything above Himself, He’d be denying the truth about His own perfection. His joy in His glory is really the bedrock of all that’s right and beautiful.
 
 Think about it. You see this story everywhere—a student working hard for approval, an artist hoping someone will notice, a friend longing to be seen. Deep down, we all chase after glory, after worth. But when we look for it in ourselves or hope to find it in others, it never really lasts. The applause fades, the spotlight moves on, and that empty feeling creeps back in.
 
 God’s glory is different. It never fades. It’s unchanging, eternal, and completely satisfying. And the amazing thing is, when you catch even a glimpse of it, your own desires start to shift. Suddenly, that hunger for approval or recognition just doesn’t have the same hold. Instead, there’s this new longing—to see and savor the beauty of God Himself.
 
 That’s where real worship comes alive. It’s not about checking boxes or following rituals. It’s about responding to God’s worth with awe and delight. When you get a taste of His glory, you can’t help but rejoice. Your heart gets drawn out of itself and into the wonder of something so much greater.
 
 And here’s the best part: this joy isn’t just God’s alone. He actually invites us in—to share in His happiness. That’s the secret a lot of people miss: real, lasting joy doesn’t come from chasing your own glory, but from delighting in His. When you make much of God, your own heart fills up with a joy nothing else can touch.
 
 Something amazing happens when you really get this. That constant drive for approval? It starts to fade. The need to prove yourself? It loses its grip. Instead, you find a new kind of freedom—a sense that life isn’t about making much of yourself, but about enjoying the One who’s truly worthy.

## Chapter 3 The Father’s Delight in the Son

Chapter 3: The Father’s Delight in the Son
 
 Imagine a dad watching his little one take those very first steps. His face lights up—not just because of what his child is doing, but because of who they are. There’s pride, love, and pure delight all mixed together. Now, think about that feeling, but on a whole new level—that’s how God the Father feels about His Son, Jesus Christ. It’s not just about achievements; it’s about the deep joy the Father finds in His Son’s very being.
 
 Here’s something amazing—this relationship between the Father and the Son is at the heart of everything. Before the world existed, before even a single star shone in the sky, the Father and Son shared a love and a joy that was complete, overflowing, and unbreakable. The Son is the perfect reflection of the Father’s glory, showing off His character and beauty in every way. When the Father looks at the Son, He sees everything good and true about Himself, perfectly echoed back.
 
 Now, let’s be honest—thinking of God as a Father isn’t always easy. Maybe you grew up with a dad who was distant, demanding, or simply not there. But the connection between the Father and the Son is entirely different. It’s full of perfect love, mutual delight, and a closeness that never breaks. This isn’t some cold, formal relationship—it’s living, joyful, and real.
 
 You know those moments in families, where the joy isn’t just about what someone does, but simply about who they are? The laughter around the dinner table, the quiet moments when you just get each other, or the comfort of being together in silence. That’s the kind of delight we’re talking about. The Father’s joy in the Son isn’t tied to performance or achievement. It comes from the very heart of who the Son is—He perfectly reflects everything the Father loves.
 
 And here’s where it gets personal. The joy the Father finds in the Son? That’s the same joy He invites you to share. When you come to know Jesus, you’re drawn right into the middle of this divine delight. You’re not left on the outside looking in—you’re welcomed as a beloved child. The love that’s always existed between the Father and the Son becomes the foundation for your own relationship with God.
 
 So many of us spend our lives chasing approval, trying to prove we’re enough. But the story of the Father and the Son flips that script. Approval isn’t something you have to earn—it’s a gift, freely given. The Father’s delight in the Son never changes, and if you’re connected to the Son by faith, you’re welcomed into that same delight.
 
 That changes everything. You’re no longer defined by your successes or failures, or by what others think of you. Your identity is rooted in the Father’s love. And worship? It becomes a joyful celebration—a way of responding to the beauty and worth of the Son, and the love that makes you part of the family.

## Chapter 4 God’s Joy in Creation and His Works

Chapter 4: God’s Joy in Creation and His Works
 
 Picture this: it’s a clear morning, sunlight pouring over the fields, and birds singing just outside your window. Maybe you remember being a kid, running barefoot through the grass, feeling like the world was something magical just waiting to be discovered. 
 
 In moments like these, you can almost feel it—creation isn’t just here for us to use... it’s a gift to be enjoyed.
 
 God’s joy in creation is woven into everything around us. When He made the heavens and the earth, it wasn’t out of boredom or because He needed to. He created because it brought Him delight. The beauty, the order, the creativity of the universe—it all reflects His glory. Think about it: every mountain, every river, every living creature is a kind of love letter from the Creator, pointing to His delight.
 
 But here’s something to pay attention to—most of us miss this in the rush of everyday life. The world can start to feel like just a backdrop, or maybe even just another problem to fix. But when you slow down and let the noise fade, that sense of wonder comes back. Notice the details: the patterns on a single leaf, the vastness of the night sky, the sound of children laughing. These are reminders that creation isn’t an accident—it’s a masterpiece, crafted with joy.
 
 Every garden, every forest, even your local park, tells a story. It’s a story of beauty, order, and purpose. Our world isn’t random or meaningless. It’s the work of a God who genuinely delights in what He has made. And that delight? It’s not far-off or theoretical. It’s right here, alive in every sunrise, every shifting season, every small act of kindness you witness.
 
 But God’s pleasure doesn’t stop with the physical world. He finds joy in His works of justice, mercy, and redemption too. When wrongs are set right, when people are freed from oppression, when broken lives are mended—God rejoices. His joy stretches beyond nature; it reaches into every life changed by His grace.
 
 Maybe you’re wondering, “But what about all the pain and injustice in the world?” That’s a real question. Sometimes, it’s hard to see where God’s joy fits when there’s suffering all around us. But here’s the hope tucked within the story of creation: God isn’t distant or indifferent to our pain. He’s at work, bringing light into dark places, healing where there’s brokenness, and weaving joy even into moments of sorrow.
 
 There’s a quiet confidence that grows when you know this. Even when life feels chaotic, you can sense that things aren’t just spinning out of control. The Creator is still here, still delighting in His creation, still working out His purposes. 
 
 And that changes everything. Suddenly, every act of beauty, every glimpse of justice, every step toward healing becomes a sign—a reminder—of God’s ongoing joy in His works.

## Chapter 5 God’s Pleasure in Justice, Love, and Redemption

Chapter Five: God’s Pleasure in Justice, Love, and Redemption.
 
 You know, in every community, there are these quiet moments when someone stands up for what’s right. Maybe it’s a neighbor who speaks out against something unfair, a friend who forgives a deep hurt, or a stranger who steps in to help—no strings attached. These moments might seem small, and they often go unnoticed, but they really do reflect something amazing about God’s own heart. They’re little glimpses of the justice, love, and redemption that bring Him joy.
 
 God’s pleasure in justice isn’t some cold demand for order. It’s the delight of a loving Father who wants His children treated with real dignity and respect. When the weak are protected, when people stand up for the oppressed, when wrongs get set right—God actually rejoices. His sense of justice isn’t about punishing just for the sake of it. It’s about restoring what’s broken, making things whole again.
 
 Think about forgiveness for a second. In every act of forgiveness, there’s a story—a relationship that’s been strained, a wound that felt too deep to heal. But then, forgiveness is offered, and something incredible happens. The weight of anger lifts, and suddenly, there’s hope for reconciliation. That’s where God’s pleasure in love becomes so real. He delights in mercy, in compassion, in seeing broken relationships restored.
 
 And God’s joy in love goes even further. It’s most powerful in His work of redemption—the way He rescues people from sin and despair. This isn’t some distant, impersonal transaction. It’s the passionate pursuit of a God who just refuses to give up on His creation. Whenever someone turns from darkness to light, whenever a life is transformed by grace, God’s heart is absolutely filled with joy.
 
 Of course, so many people struggle to believe this. Maybe you’ve felt the weight of guilt, or the fear that you’re just too far gone. But the story of redemption is really a story of relentless love. God isn’t looking for reasons to condemn—He’s looking for every opportunity to save. He takes pleasure in showing mercy, in welcoming the lost, in making all things new.
 
 And when you really take this in, it changes how you see yourself—and everyone else. No one is beyond the reach of God’s love. No failure is final, and no wound is too deep. The invitation is always open: receive forgiveness, extend mercy, and join in the work of justice and restoration.
 
 So worship, when you look at it this way, becomes so much more than just singing or saying prayers. It turns into a way of life—a response to the love and justice of God. It’s about choosing to join in His joy, delighting in what delights Him, and becoming agents of His redemption in the world.

## Chapter 7 The Joy of God in Obedience and Faith

Chapter 7: The Joy of God in Obedience and Faith
 
 You know that feeling when you've done the right thing, even if nobody else ever finds out? Maybe you finish a job with extra care, keep a promise to a friend, or choose honesty, even when it’d be easier to just look the other way. There’s a quiet kind of dignity in those moments.
 
 Now, imagine this on a deeper level—God actually finds joy in the obedience and faith of His people.
 
 For a lot of us, the word "obedience" might make us think about rules or restrictions. It can sound like a chore, or just another checklist of dos and don’ts. But when you look at it through the lens of God’s pleasure, obedience transforms into something else entirely. It isn’t about trying to win approval or stay out of trouble. It’s about trust—a loving response to love itself.
 
 Think about someone who chooses the tougher road. Maybe they forgive when it’d be easier to stay angry. Or give generously, even when money’s tight. Or stand up for truth, even if it means risking something. Most of the time, these acts go unnoticed. Sometimes, they’re even misunderstood. But they matter—a lot. Each one is a quiet act of faith, a small seed of trust planted in the everyday soil of life.
 
 Most people don’t notice these moments—but God does. His joy isn’t about our perfection. It’s about a heart that keeps turning toward Him, time after time. Obedience is really a way of saying, “I trust You. I believe Your way is good.” It’s not about getting everything right—it’s about staying in relationship, listening, and responding.
 
 And then there’s faith. Faith is another source of God’s delight. It’s that quiet confidence that God is exactly who He says He is, even when life gets tough. It’s the courage to step out, to take a risk, to keep hoping. There’s a beauty to faith that you can’t measure by outward success. It’s the beauty of trust—of leaning into the unknown, knowing that God is faithful.
 
 When you see obedience and faith this way, everything shifts. They aren’t burdens you have to carry—they’re gifts you can give. They’re ways to share in God’s own joy, to join in the story He’s writing. 
 
 So, worship becomes more than just words or rituals. It’s a whole life lived in response to love—a daily offering of trust and obedience.

## Chapter 8 God’s Joy in Prayer and Communion

In moments like these, you feel a closeness—like all the barriers have faded away, and you’re really connecting. That’s the kind of closeness God finds joy in when we pray and spend time with Him.

It isn’t about checking the right boxes or saying the right words. It’s about being known, being heard. God isn’t far-off or uninterested. Maybe there’s just silence, or a sigh that says more than words ever could. That’s precious to God. He doesn’t care about fancy speeches—He listens for honesty. He welcomes our stammering, our pauses, our deepest sighs.
 
 What really stands out is that prayer isn’t about getting everything perfect. It’s about drawing near, sharing life with God. You bring your joys, your disappointments, your dreams, your heartaches. And in those honest moments, something changes. The weight you’re carrying feels lighter. The loneliness eases. Hope starts to feel possible again.
 
 Sometimes, it’s easy to slip into thinking God is too busy or too distant to care about what’s on your mind. But the truth is so much more tender than that. God’s heart is actually moved by our prayers. He delights in our trust, in those moments when we choose to come close. Prayer isn’t some transaction or business deal. It’s a relationship.
 
 And when you start to see prayer that way, everything changes. It’s no longer about performing, or using the right words, or turning to God only when things fall apart. It becomes a way of living—a steady rhythm of conversation, a daily habit of drawing near. 
 
 Worship isn’t limited to a building or a set time. It’s the ongoing, everyday sharing of life with the One who always listens and delights in you.
