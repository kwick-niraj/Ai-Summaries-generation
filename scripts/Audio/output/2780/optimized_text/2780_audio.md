## Chapter 3 The Seven Essential Questions—A Practical Framework for Everyday Coaching

Let’s dive into Chapter 3—The Seven Essential Questions: A Practical Framework for Everyday Coaching.
 
 If you want coaching to feel natural in your day-to-day leadership, it really helps to have a framework you can rely on. That’s where the seven essential questions come in. They’re simple, memorable, and—most importantly—they actually work. Each one plays a unique role, guiding your conversations so they’re both effective and truly meaningful.
 
 Let’s walk through each question, and I’ll show you how you can use them in real life.
 
 **First up is the Kickstart Question:** “What’s on your mind?”
 
 This one’s a game-changer. It opens the door to real, honest conversation. You’re showing you care about what matters most to the other person. It’s an invitation—no agenda, just genuine curiosity. When you start with this, you set the stage for a safe, productive coaching session.
 
 The second question is the AWE Question: “And what else?”
 
 Funny how often the first answer you get is only scratching the surface, isn’t it? By asking “And what else?” you’re encouraging them to dig deeper, to look at things from another angle. It’s a gentle nudge that can reveal hidden issues or even spark new ideas—plus, it keeps you from jumping to conclusions too quickly.
 
 Next comes the Focus Question: “What’s the real challenge here for you?”
 
 Problems hardly ever come wrapped in a neat little box. This question helps get past the obvious and zero in on what’s really at stake. By adding “for you,” it also helps the other person take ownership and reflect on their personal stake in the issue.
 
 The fourth is the Foundation Question: “What do you want?”
 
 It sounds simple, but you’d be surprised how tough it can be for people to answer honestly. Getting clear on what someone truly wants is the key to finding solutions that actually matter. This question cuts through the noise and brings real focus.
 
 Now, the fifth question is called the Lazy Question: “How can I help?”
 
 We all like to jump in and be helpful, right? But sometimes we assume we know what’s needed—when in reality, it’s better to just ask. This puts the ball back in their court, making sure you’re actually giving the support they want, not just what you think they need.
 
 The sixth question is the Strategic Question: “If you’re saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?”
 
 Every choice comes with trade-offs. By asking this, you help them look at priorities and think through the real costs or consequences. It’s a reality check that leads to smarter, more intentional decisions.
 
 And finally, the seventh question: the Learning Question—“What was most useful for you?”
 
 Reflection is where the magic happens. This question gives the other person a chance to recognize what they’ve learned and take something valuable away from the conversation. It leaves them feeling more confident and encourages growth.
 
 Now, don’t think you have to use these seven questions in a strict order. The real art of coaching is listening—really listening—and picking the right question for the moment. Go with the flow, and let the conversation guide you.
 
 You can use these questions anywhere—one-on-one meetings, quick chats in the hallway, even in team discussions. With practice, they’ll become second nature, helping you build a culture where curiosity, learning, and empowerment thrive.
 
 And here’s what’s really cool: leaders who use these questions regularly see their teams become more engaged, creative, and capable. Problems get solved in fresh ways, and people feel heard and valued.
 
 So, the seven essential questions aren’t just coaching tools—they’re building blocks for a more human, effective approach to leadership.
