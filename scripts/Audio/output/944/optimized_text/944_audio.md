## Introduction

Have you ever noticed how childhood seems so different these days? Instead of the freedom to roam outside, so many kids find themselves tethered to screens and packed schedules. It's easy to forget how much wonder, creativity, and resilience used to come from simple, unstructured adventures in nature.
 
 There's even a name for what happens when we drift too far from the wild places that shape us—it's called "nature-deficit disorder." It’s a powerful way to describe what’s lost when physical play gives way to indoor routines, and when the spark of curiosity dims just a little with each passing day.
 
 In his groundbreaking book, Last Child in the Woods: Saving Our Children from Nature-Deficit Disorder, Richard Louv invites us to look closer at how this shift is affecting not just our children, but all of us. He weaves together heartfelt stories, fascinating research, and cultural snapshots, showing how reconnecting with the natural world can nurture confidence, creativity, and a true sense of belonging.
 
 As we explore these ideas together, you’ll discover why restoring our connection with nature isn’t just a nostalgic dream—it’s essential for the health of our kids, our communities, and our future. 
 So, let’s set out on this journey to rediscover the joys and the deep benefits of the natural world, and imagine what’s possible when we bring childhood—and ourselves—back outside.
