 Another approach is **establishing routines**. Having set patterns for daily stuff—like meal planning or your morning ritual—means you don’t have to reinvent the wheel every day. Routines are like mental shortcuts, saving your brainpower for bigger decisions.
 
 And then there’s **setting boundaries**. Decide ahead of time what’s worth your attention. Maybe you always order the same coffee at your favorite café. That way, you’re not wasting precious mental energy on tiny, repeat choices.
 
 Now, this isn’t about controlling every part of your life. It’s about making space for what’s truly important. When you automate or simplify the small stuff, you create room for creativity, connection, and real joy.
 
 Picture someone who dreads deciding what to cook every night. If they plan their meals for the whole week, suddenly that daily panic disappears. Cooking becomes less stressful—and more enjoyable.
 
 You can also use checklists or simple frameworks for decisions you face all the time. Let’s say you’re about to buy something. Ask yourself: Do I actually need this, or just want it?
