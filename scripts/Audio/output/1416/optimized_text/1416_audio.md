Maybe for you, that means keeping lots of books because you love each one. Or maybe it means letting go of most things except for a few truly special pieces.

 And here’s where the joy principle goes even deeper. It’s not just for your stuff. As you tidy, you might notice you start applying this question—does it spark joy?—to other parts of your life.  **First**, pick up each item and really notice how it makes you feel.

 When you let joy guide what stays, tidying up shifts from being about giving things up, to celebrating the things that make your life richer.
 
 In the end, you’re left with a home—and a life—that’s full of meaning, comfort, and real happiness.

## Chapter 3 Organizing by Category: A Systematic Approach

**Chapter 3: Organizing by Category—A Systematic Approach**
 
 Let’s take a closer look at one of the most powerful shifts in this method: moving from tidying room by room, to tidying by category. 
 
 Now, most of us are pretty used to the idea of tackling the kitchen one day, the bedroom the next. But have you ever noticed how frustrating that can be? It’s like you’re chasing clutter from one corner to another—because similar items are scattered all over the place. You never really get the full picture, and making decisions feels nearly impossible.
 
 Here’s where the magic happens. Instead of jumping from space to space, you gather every single item from one category—like all your clothes, or all your books—into a single spot. Imagine seeing every piece of clothing you own, all at once. Suddenly, it’s way easier to spot duplicates, realize what you’ve forgotten, and notice where you might have a little… or a lot… too much.
 
 This approach isn’t just about getting organized—it’s about understanding yourself. You start to notice patterns in what you buy, what you hold onto, and what genuinely matters to you. And honestly, that can be pretty eye-opening.
 
 Now, there’s a recommended order for tackling these categories, and it’s designed to help you build your confidence and decision-making skills as you go. Here’s how it usually goes:
 
 Clothes come first—they’re usually the easiest because, let’s face it, you know what you wear and what just sits in the back of the closet. Next up are books, which can be a little trickier, especially if you love to read or collect. Papers follow, and while they can feel overwhelming, most of them are probably things you no longer need. Then there’s miscellaneous stuff—think kitchen gadgets, hobby supplies, those odds and ends we all have. And finally, sentimental items—photos, keepsakes, things that tug at your heartstrings—these come last, when you’re ready to make those tougher calls.
 
 Each category brings its own set of challenges. Clothes can be surprisingly straightforward. Books might make you hesitate, especially if you’re attached to them. Papers often just need a good sort and a recycling bin. Miscellaneous items? That’s where the “just in case” trap likes to hide. And sentimental things—well, those deserve a special kind of attention.
 
 But no matter the category, the process stays the same: gather everything together, pick up each item one by one, and ask yourself—does this spark joy? 
 
 This simple, consistent approach means nothing gets missed, and you avoid the common pitfall of just shuffling things from one room to another. You’re not just moving clutter—you’re actually addressing it.
 
 And here’s another bonus: organizing by category makes storage so much easier. Once you know exactly what you’re keeping, you can decide the best way to store it, so it’s easy to see and grab whenever you need it. The goal? Make it simple to find and return each item, and you’ll dramatically cut down on future clutter.
 
 A few practical tips to keep in mind as you go:
 
 First, always gather every item in a category before making decisions. Follow the recommended order—start with what’s easiest, and work your way up. Take the time to handle each piece individually, and really focus on the feeling it brings you. Finally, store things so they’re visible and accessible—it’ll make your life so much easier.
 
 So, by shifting your focus from rooms to categories, tidying suddenly feels a whole lot more manageable. Plus, you’ll get a deeper understanding of your habits, your preferences, and what truly matters in your life. That’s the real foundation for change that lasts.
