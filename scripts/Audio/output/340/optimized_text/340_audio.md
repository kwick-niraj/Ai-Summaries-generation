## Chapter 2 Breaking the Cycle of Excuses—Moving Beyond Self-Imposed Limits

 There’s actually a name for this pattern: some folks call it “excuse-itis.” It’s that persistent little voice whispering, “I’d give it a shot, but...” or, “People like me just don’t win at that.” If we let that voice take over, it turns into a self-fulfilling prophecy—shrinking our idea of what’s possible, step by step.
 
 Here’s something to pay attention to: the most common types of excuses usually sound like one of these...
 
 Health excuses—blaming a lack of progress on physical limits, whether they’re real or just something we believe.
 
 Intelligence or education excuses—assuming that only people with fancy degrees or special talent can really succeed.
 
 Age excuses—telling ourselves it’s too late, or we’re just too young to go after what we want.
 
 And then there are luck excuses—believing that everything comes down to chance, not effort or strategy.
 
 But here’s the truth: every single one of these can be challenged just by shifting how we look at things. Think about it—history is full of people who did incredible things despite health issues, limited education, or starting way later than everyone else. The real key? Focusing on what you can actually control. Things like your attitude, your effort, and how much you’re willing to learn.
 
 So, how do you start breaking out of that cycle of excuses? Try this:

 Next, when you hit a challenge, try reframing it as a chance to learn or grow, instead of a reason to throw in the towel.

 Here’s the key takeaway: excuses only have power when we let them hide in the shadows. When we call them out and take real, constructive action, those walls start to crumble.
