## Chapter 2 Recognizing Overload and Deciding What to Delegate

 - Specialized work that doesn’t play to your strengths—think advanced web development or digging into legal documents.
 
 - Or anything that eats up a ton of time but doesn’t really drive your business forward.
 
 Here’s a simple framework to make things actionable:
 
 First, list out all the tasks you handle in a typical week. Next, split them into two buckets—core tasks that are at the heart of your service or product, and non-core tasks that are more about support or admin.  But this isn’t just about being efficient. It’s about protecting your energy and creativity for the work that matters most. By letting go of those draining tasks, you free yourself up to do what you do best—whether it’s designing, writing, consulting, or building something new.
 
 Let’s look at a real-world example. Picture a freelance graphic designer who spends hours each week on client emails and invoicing. Or think about a small business owner bogged down by social media. Handing off content scheduling and analytics to a specialist means more time to focus on developing new products.
 
 Choosing to delegate isn’t just a practical decision—it’s a mindset shift. It means letting go of the belief that you have to do everything yourself, and embracing the idea that working with others can actually help you achieve more. **That** shift is key to growing your business in a sustainable, healthy way.
