 Even when you’re trying your best to think things through, your mind has its own way of taking shortcuts. These mental shortcuts—what psychologists call cognitive biases—are designed to help us make decisions quickly. But as handy as they are, they can just as easily trip us up.

Or maybe you’ve fallen for anchoring, where that very first piece of information you hear sticks in your mind and colors everything else. Then there’s availability bias—where you give more weight to stuff that comes to mind easily, whether it’s actually important or not.
 
 These mental habits are kind of like walking down a familiar path in the woods. It’s comfortable, almost automatic. But sometimes, that path doesn’t actually lead you where you want to go.
 
 So how do you break free and make better choices? Here are a few strategies that really work:
 
 First, try deliberately searching out information that challenges your current point of view. It’s not always fun, but it can really open your eyes.

 One thing to keep in mind: nobody is immune to bias. Intelligence, experience, none of that makes you bulletproof. The trick isn’t to try to get rid of bias completely—because, honestly, that’s impossible. Instead, it’s about noticing when bias creeps in, and putting up a few guardrails.
