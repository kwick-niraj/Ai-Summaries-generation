## Chapter 2 Mapping the Decision Landscape: Identifying Key Stakeholders

Let’s dive into Chapter 2: **Mapping the Decision Landscape—Identifying Key Stakeholders.**
 
 Once you start seeing sales as more than just convincing one person, things really start to shift. Instead of focusing on a single decision-maker, you begin to look at the bigger picture—how organizations actually make big choices.
 In complex sales, it’s almost never just a matter of one person saying “yes” or “no.” 
 
 Decisions are shaped by a whole network of people, each playing their own part, each bringing their own perspective and influence.
 
 So, how do you navigate this landscape? Well, it helps to think in terms of four key “buying influences.” 
 These are the folks whose opinions, needs, and authority will decide whether your solution makes the cut.
 
 Let’s break them down:
 
 First up, there’s the **Economic Buyer.** This is the person holding the budget—and the final say when it comes to spending. What matters most to them? It’s all about the return on investment. They want to know: will this really deliver value for the money?
 
 Next, we have the **User Buyers.** These are the people who’ll actually be using your product or service every day. They’re thinking about practicality, ease of use, and how it’ll impact their workflow.
 
 The **Technical Buyer** comes in third. This group checks if your solution fits technical standards, compliance, or policies. They’re the gatekeepers—focused on compatibility, risk, and making sure nothing falls through the cracks.
 
 And finally, there’s the **Coach.** This is your insider. The person who helps you understand the organization’s dynamics. They’ll give you a heads up about potential roadblocks and quietly advocate for your solution behind the scenes.
 
 Now, sometimes one person might wear more than one of these hats; other times, these roles could be spread across different departments. The real trick is figuring out who’s who in your current opportunity.
 
 Let’s look at a real-world example. Imagine a company thinking about upgrading to a new logistics system. The **Economic Buyer** could be the CFO, laser-focused on cost savings. The **User Buyers** might be the warehouse managers, who care about how their daily routines could be disrupted. The **Technical Buyer** could be the IT security officer, worried about protecting sensitive data. And that **Coach?** Maybe it’s a project manager who’s worked with you before and wants to see the project succeed.
 
 But here’s something to pay attention to—identifying these roles isn’t always straightforward. Job titles can be misleading. Sometimes, the real decision-maker isn’t the one with the biggest title, but the person with the most trust and credibility.
 
 So, how can you get a clear picture of who’s involved? Here are a few ways:
 
 Ask open-ended questions about how the decision will be made.
 
 Listen carefully for hints about who’s really influencing things, and what matters most to them.
 
 Watch the group dynamics during meetings and email threads—who’s driving the conversation?
 
 And if you can, find a coach inside the organization who’ll give you the lay of the land.
 
 By mapping out these key players and understanding their roles, you can tailor your approach to address each person’s unique concerns. And when you do that, you’re not just improving your odds of success—you’re building trust and credibility that will serve you well in every deal.
