Many found themselves pushed aside, their voices drowned out by the demands of the new state.
 
 Still, even with all these struggles, hope didn’t vanish overnight. The Soviet Union made it through the civil war and began to rebuild. Factories reopened, schools sprang up, and the country slowly started to recover. Many believed the worst was behind them, and that the promise of the revolution could still come true.
 
 But underneath all that progress, new problems were taking root. The very institutions that were meant to protect the revolution were turning into sources of power for a whole new elite. The dream of a society run by workers was quietly shifting—turning into a world run by bureaucrats.
 
 As the 1920s drew to a close, it was clear that the revolution stood at a crossroads. The choices made in these years would shape not just the future of the Soviet Union, but the fate of socialism itself.

The revolution had promised democracy, equality, and genuine participation. But now, those things were being pushed aside by hierarchy, privilege, and control.
